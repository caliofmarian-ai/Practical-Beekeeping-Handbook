Status: planned for final layout. No provisional artwork is embedded in the reader-facing manuscript.

## Planned Figures

### Figure 63.1 — Brand Is More Than a Logo
Central customer expectation surrounded by product quality, name, visual identity, origin, service, packaging, delivery, claims and reputation; logo shown as one component.

### Figure 63.2 — Name / Company / Domain / Social / Trade Mark
Five distinct but overlapping identity assets with clear captions explaining that one does not automatically create rights in the others.

### Figure 63.3 — Brand Naming Funnel
Naming brief → candidate names → language/domain/basic conflict search → professional trade-mark review where justified → selected name → packaging investment.

### Figure 63.4 — Positioning Map
Illustrative axes such as everyday/premium and traditional/modern, showing the need for a clear position rather than occupying every space.

### Figure 63.5 — Brand Proof Pyramid
Customer promise supported by traceability, certification, laboratory evidence, apiary/harvest records and service standards.

### Figure 63.6 — Visual Identity System
Logo, typography, colour, iconography, photography and label architecture shown as a coordinated system rather than independent decoration.

### Figure 63.7 — Small-Surface Test
Same logo/identity on full website, honey jar, lid, small social avatar and monochrome carton stamp, illustrating the need for legibility at real sizes.

### Figure 63.8 — Packaging Information Hierarchy
Front/side/back pack layout distinguishing brand identity, legal product name, origin, net quantity and mandatory information from optional storytelling.

### Figure 63.9 — Brand Architecture
Master brand branching to product families such as honey, live bees/breeding, hive products and services; alternative sub-brand model shown only where justified.

### Figure 63.10 — Claim Library
Approved claim wording linked to evidence, legal basis/certification, products and review/expiry date; unapproved health/origin/environmental claims blocked.

### Figure 63.11 — Truthful Origin Cues
Traceable local honey with matching map/origin cues versus an imported blend with misleading local flag imagery. Caption: visual impression must agree with legal origin.

### Figure 63.12 — Digital Brand Asset Security
Domain registrar, website, email and social accounts protected by business-controlled MFA, password management and recovery contacts.

### Figure 63.13 — Rebrand Cost Map
Name/logo change radiating into labels, cartons, signs, website, domains, wholesale documents, uniforms and IP filings.

### Figure 63.14 — Brand Equity Measures
Recognition → trust/preference → repeat purchase → referrals/retailer demand → sustainable price/margin, showing why awareness alone is insufficient.

## Accuracy Requirements

- Do not imply domain/business-name registration equals trade-mark ownership.
- Trade-mark examples must remain jurisdiction-neutral and refer readers to professional/current local advice where needed.
- Mandatory food information must be visibly legible in packaging diagrams.
- Local/floral/organic/sustainability/health visual claims must be tied to evidence.
- Scientific/medical imagery must not imply therapeutic properties of ordinary foods.
- Customer photography must include permission/rights control.

## Accessibility

- Use labels, patterns and shapes in addition to colour.
- Packaging hierarchy must work in greyscale.
- Brand-positioning and equity figures should have text labels at every key point.
