# Chapter 74 — Future of Beekeeping: Illustration Plan

Status: planned for final layout. No provisional artwork is embedded in the reader-facing manuscript.

## Planned Figures

### Figure 74.1 — Five-, Ten-, and Twenty-Year Scenario Horizons
Three non-deterministic scenario bands showing near-, medium-, and long-term possibilities for climate, invasive pests, breeding, automation, regulation, markets, and labour. Caption must emphasise scenarios, not predictions.

### Figure 74.2 — Technology-Adoption Framework
Decision flow: real problem → evidence of benefit → validation in local conditions → total cost of ownership → interoperability/data ownership → failure mode → human override → adoption/rejection/retest.

### Figure 74.3 — Precision Apiary Architecture
Hive sensors, weather station, scale, acoustic/temperature data, gateway, cloud/local analytics, beekeeper dashboard, and manual inspection feedback loop. Show technology augmenting—not replacing—physical inspection.

### Figure 74.4 — AI Decision Support Versus Autonomous Action
Side-by-side pathways distinguishing advisory risk scoring from automated action. Include confidence, false-positive/false-negative, and human verification gates.

### Figure 74.5 — Climate Adaptation Toolbox
Phenology records, shade/water, forage diversification, queen/stock selection, migration flexibility, heat/flood/fire planning, and revised seasonal calendars.

### Figure 74.6 — Emerging Pest Pathway
Conceptual spread pathway for an exotic parasite such as Tropilaelaps: surveillance → detection → confirmation → movement control → regional response → long-term endemic management if establishment occurs. Do not hard-code geographic forecasts.

### Figure 74.7 — Breeding and Genomic Selection
Traditional phenotype selection linked with pedigree, field survival, hygienic traits, genomic/marker information, and repeated field validation.

### Figure 74.8 — Reduced-Chemical-Dependence IPM
Integrated pest-management wheel combining monitoring, resistant stock, brood manipulation, biotechnical controls, authorised treatments, resistance rotation, and verification.

### Figure 74.9 — Molecular Diagnostics Pipeline
Field sample → validated molecular assay → result with uncertainty → management interpretation → regulatory/clinical context. Emphasise that detection is not automatically causation.

### Figure 74.10 — Honey Authenticity and Digital Traceability
Apiary lot → extraction batch → analytical fingerprint → digital lot record → blend mass balance → pack → customer/recall traceability. Keep blockchain/advanced ledger technology optional rather than mandatory.

### Figure 74.11 — Automation and Robotics Opportunities
Examples: pallet handling, remote monitoring, automated feeding only where appropriate, honey-room automation, vision inspection, and logistics. Pair each with cost, maintenance and failure-risk icons.

### Figure 74.12 — Data Governance Map
Data sources → owner/operator → vendor/cloud → processor/customer/regulator. Show permissions, retention, portability, cybersecurity and vendor-lock-in considerations.

### Figure 74.13 — Future Business Models
Small local premium producer, cooperative/shared processing, pollination specialist, commercial migratory operator, breeder/queen producer, data-enabled service provider, and diversified hive-products business. No ranking implied.

### Figure 74.14 — Future-Readiness Checklist
Resilience categories: biology, finance, regulation, climate, technology, market, workforce, biodiversity, and records. Designed as an audit figure for periodic review.

## Accuracy Requirements

- All future-facing visuals must be framed as scenarios or trends, not certainties.
- Do not depict AI/automation as replacing beekeeper observation or regulatory responsibility.
- Geographic pest-spread figures must be dated and avoid deterministic forecasts.
- Genomic selection must remain linked to phenotype and field validation.
- Digital traceability must not imply that blockchain is required for authenticity.
- Technology panels must include cost, maintenance, interoperability, cybersecurity and failure modes.
- Climate figures must avoid claiming one universal effect across all regions.

## Accessibility

- Use labels, patterns and icons in addition to colour.
- Scenario charts must remain understandable in greyscale.
- Technology diagrams should use plain-language captions for non-specialist readers.
