# Chapter 27 — Emergency and Winter Feeding: Illustration Plan

## Status

Planned for the final illustration and layout stage.

## Purpose

The visuals should explain cold-weather food access, starvation recognition, feed placement, moisture control and emergency decision-making.

## Visual 1 — Winter Cluster and Accessible Stores

### Format

Accurate hive cross-section.

### Content

Show:

- Winter cluster spanning several combs.
- warmer central area.
- cooler outer layer.
- honey immediately above and beside the cluster.
- empty comb beyond the cluster.
- normal upward food movement.

### Accuracy requirements

- Realistic frame spacing.
- realistic cluster shape.
- no implication that the entire hive is heated.

## Visual 2 — Isolation Starvation

### Format

Side-by-side comb arrangement.

### Safe arrangement

Show food directly above and beside the cluster.

### Dangerous arrangement

Show:

- Small cluster.
- empty comb barrier.
- substantial honey several frames away.
- brood anchoring the cluster.
- prolonged cold preventing lateral movement.

### Message

Total food does not guarantee accessible food.

## Visual 3 — Winter Feed Types

### Format

Comparative plate.

### Content

Show:

- Heavy syrup for suitable autumn conditions.
- bee fondant.
- candy board.
- dry granulated sugar.
- sugar brick.
- healthy capped food frame.

For each feed, show:

- Appropriate general conditions.
- placement.
- principal advantage.
- principal limitation.

## Visual 4 — Fondant Placement

### Format

Detailed upper-hive cross-section.

### Content

Show:

- Cluster below top bars.
- fondant directly above the occupied area.
- access opening facing bees.
- shallow rim.
- insulation above.
- secure roof.
- no empty box separating feed and cluster.

## Visual 5 — Incorrect Feed Placement

### Format

Error-identification illustration.

### Show

- Fondant placed above an empty super.
- cold syrup feeder beyond the cluster.
- candy blocked by an inner cover.
- food frame several frames away.
- insulation covering the access route.
- leaking feeder.

## Visual 6 — Hive Weight Monitoring

### Format

Step-by-step practical illustration.

### Content

Show:

- Rear hefting.
- side weighing with luggage scale.
- two-side weighing.
- full platform scale.
- record chart showing gradual weight decline.

### Safety

Show stable footing and minimal lifting.

## Visual 7 — Condensation and Moisture

### Format

Two hive cross-sections.

### Unsafe hive

Show:

- Cold roof.
- condensation droplets.
- water falling onto cluster.
- soaked insulation.
- level floor retaining water.

### Improved hive

Show:

- appropriate roof insulation.
- controlled ventilation.
- drainage.
- dry cluster area.
- moisture directed away from bees.

## Visual 8 — Emergency Feeding Sequence

### Format

Numbered instructional sequence.

### Steps

1. Assess weight and weather.
2. prepare solid feed.
3. locate cluster.
4. open only the upper cover.
5. place feed directly above bees.
6. restore insulation.
7. close and secure hive.
8. record and monitor.

### Content

- unsuitable placement that divides the cluster.
- correct frame spacing.

### Format

Four small apiary scenes.

## Visual 12 — Winter Feeding Decision Chart

### Format

Professional flowchart.

### Decisions

- Is the colony alive?
- Is it viable?
- Are stores sufficient?
- Are stores accessible?
- Can liquid feed be processed?
- Is solid feed required?
- Is disease or queen failure present?
- Is feed within cluster reach?
- When should monitoring occur?

## Style Direction

- Professional scientific handbook style.
- Realistic honey bees and equipment.
- Accurate seasonal conditions.
- Consistent hive perspective.
- Clear labels.
- No cartoon anthropomorphism.
- No commercial branding.
- No decorative elements that reduce clarity.
- Suitable for print and digital editions.

## Production Notes

The visuals should be created during final book layout together with the complete illustration system so that typography, line weight, colour treatment, captions and dimensions remain consistent.
