## Planned Figures

### Figure 25.1 — Inspection Depth Ladder
External observation → limited check → targeted inspection → full inspection → emergency inspection, with the information need increasing alongside colony disturbance.

### Figure 25.2 — Pre-Opening Observation
Entrance view showing normal flight, pollen return, orientation flights, fanning and bearding alongside clearly labelled warning patterns such as fighting/robbing, abnormal mortality and blocked entrance.

### Figure 25.3 — Inspection Questions Before Opening
Decision card with the core questions: queenright? brood appropriate? food adequate? enough space? pest/disease/equipment warning signs? action today? next inspection when?

### Figure 25.4 — Safe Frame-Handling Sequence
Loosen frame, create working space, lift vertically, hold over the hive, rotate without rolling bees, inspect both faces and return to the same orientation. Highlight queen-crushing risk at frame edges.

### Figure 25.5 — Queenright Evidence Without Finding the Queen
Eggs, very young larvae and coherent brood development shown as evidence supporting recent queen function, contrasted with the unnecessary disruption of searching continuously for the queen.

### Figure 25.6 — Healthy Brood-Age Reference
Eggs, young larvae, older larvae and capped worker brood in realistic cells, providing a normal developmental reference rather than a disease-diagnosis plate.

### Figure 25.7 — Brood Pattern Interpretation
Coherent brood with normal gaps versus irregular brood that triggers further investigation. Caption lists possible causes—queen, disease, Varroa, nutrition, chilling—without diagnosing from pattern alone.

### Figure 25.8 — Colony Strength Estimate
Frames substantially covered by adult bees plus brood-area assessment, illustrating why adult population, brood and food should be interpreted together rather than from one frame count alone.

### Figure 25.9 — Food-Reserve Assessment
Pollen, open nectar, capped honey and brood-nest position shown on representative combs, with accessible food distinguished from remote stores that may be difficult for a small cold cluster to reach.

### Figure 25.10 — Space and Congestion
Adequate laying/storage room compared with brood-nest backfilling, crowded seams, full supers and insufficient usable comb. Avoid treating bearding alone as proof of swarming.

### Figure 25.11 — Swarm-Preparation Evidence
Queen cups, charged queen cells with egg/larva, more advanced queen cells and brood-nest congestion, with a clear distinction between evidence requiring management and empty cups alone.

### Figure 25.12 — Warning Signs Requiring Targeted Follow-Up
Deformed-wing bee, suspicious brood, severe robbing, unusual mortality, feeder leak, damaged equipment and possible queen failure, each linked to the appropriate later chapter rather than an image-only diagnosis.

### Figure 25.13 — Inspection Record and Follow-Up Loop
Observation → objective record → decision/action → next inspection date → verify outcome, demonstrating that an inspection is incomplete without a follow-up plan.

### Figure 25.14 — Closing the Hive Without Crushing Bees
Smoke/brush only as needed, clear box edges, lower components carefully, maintain bee space and secure roof/entrance before leaving.

## Accuracy Requirements

- Pollen return must not be shown as proof of a healthy laying queen.
- Brood pattern and odour must not be presented as stand-alone diagnoses.
- Orientation flights must be visually distinguished from robbing.
- Bearding must not be equated automatically with swarm preparation.
- Frame-handling geometry must be realistic and minimise queen/bee crushing.
- Inspection-frequency graphics must not impose a universal calendar interval; depth/frequency depend on objective, season, weather and previous findings.

## Accessibility and Layout

- Use labels, arrow directions and pattern differences in addition to colour.
- Normal-versus-warning panels must work in greyscale.
- Close-up brood illustrations require consistent magnification cues.
- Captions should state what an observation supports and what it cannot prove.
