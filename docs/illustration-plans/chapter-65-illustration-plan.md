## Planned Figures

### Figure 65.1 — Farmers-Market Business Model
Flow from stock preparation → market setup → customer interaction → transaction → stock/lot reconciliation → repeat customer/online follow-up, with profitability and traceability running through the entire process.

### Figure 65.2 — Market-Day Contribution
Simple waterfall showing revenue reduced by product cost, pitch fee, transport, card fees, sampling, breakage and labour to reach event contribution.

### Figure 65.3 — Safe Stall Layout
Top-view plan showing customer display, reserve stock off ground, sampling zone, payment area, waste, personal belongings and clear access path.

### Figure 65.4 — Canopy and Weather Safety
Properly weighted canopy, protected stock and cable routing contrasted with unstable tie-downs, wind exposure and rain entering food/electrical areas.

### Figure 65.5 — Product Display Hierarchy
Distance views showing brand/category first, product/price second and detailed education closer to the table.

### Figure 65.6 — Sampling Hygiene Workflow
Covered source product → single-use utensil → customer → immediate waste disposal, with separate payment/food-handling and allergen-control cues.

### Figure 65.7 — Glass Breakage Response
Broken jar → isolate area → discard exposed open food/samples → collect glass → clean/inspect → reopen only when safe.

### Figure 65.8 — Lot Traceability at a Temporary Stall
Warehouse lot → labelled transport crate → market stock sheet/POS → sold SKU/lot → remaining stock → recall path.

### Figure 65.9 — Market Performance Dashboard
Contribution, contribution per labour hour, conversion rate, average transaction value, sell-through, units/transaction and repeat/wholesale leads.

### Figure 65.10 — Market Comparison Matrix
Several event examples plotted by commercial return, labour burden and strategic value, illustrating why gross revenue alone is insufficient.

### Figure 65.11 — Customer-Question Feedback Loop
Repeated market questions → FAQ/label/site improvements → better conversion and fewer misunderstandings.

### Figure 65.12 — Open-Food Risk Versus Sealed Product
Sealed jars with relatively simple hygiene needs compared with open sampling requiring hand hygiene, utensil, allergen, waste and contamination controls.

### Figure 65.13 — Market-Day Timeline
Loading → travel → setup → trading checks → close-down → stock reconciliation → equipment cleaning → event review.

## Accuracy Requirements

- Do not present organiser acceptance as proof that all legal registrations/permissions are complete.
- Sampling figures must show no double dipping and protected open food.
- Honey must not be shown being offered to infants under 12 months.
- Health/disease-treatment sales claims must not appear in signage examples.
- Traceability must remain intact through temporary retail stock handling.
- Safety figures must address wind, glass, electricity and trip hazards without relying only on colour coding.

## Accessibility

- Use labels, icons and patterns in addition to colour.
- Financial charts must remain understandable in greyscale.
- Stall-layout diagrams should use clear text labels and accessible contrast.
