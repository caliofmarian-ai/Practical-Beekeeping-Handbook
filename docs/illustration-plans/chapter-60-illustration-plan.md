# Chapter 60 — Starting a Beekeeping Business: Illustration Plan

Status: planned for final layout. No provisional artwork is embedded in the reader-facing manuscript.

## Planned Figures

### Figure 60.1 — Beekeeping Business System
Colonies/forage → production → processing → storage → sales channel → customer → cash/records feeding back into colony and business planning.

### Figure 60.2 — Revenue Streams and Biological Inputs
Honey, queens/nuclei, pollen, royal jelly, wax, pollination, and services mapped against colony resources, labour, equipment, and compliance requirements.

### Figure 60.3 — Bottleneck Principle
Several capacity bars—colonies, labour, extraction, storage, packing, sales—with the smallest capacity visibly limiting total output.

### Figure 60.4 — Revenue, Margin, Profit, and Cash Flow
Simple waterfall diagram separating sales revenue, direct costs, operating costs, capital/debt effects, and timing of cash receipts/payments.

### Figure 60.5 — Working-Capital Season
Season-neutral cash-flow curve showing expenses before harvest/sales and the need for a cash buffer.

### Figure 60.6 — Poor / Expected / Strong Season Scenarios
Three parallel forecasts varying crop, colony loss, and sales rather than relying on one optimistic number.

### Figure 60.7 — Unit Economics
Example product/colony metrics: cost/kg honey, margin/jar, profit/production colony, contribution/nucleus, labour per revenue unit.

### Figure 60.8 — Break-Even
Fixed-cost line and contribution per unit leading to break-even volume. Keep numerical examples generic and illustrative.

### Figure 60.9 — Compliance Map
Food-business registration, hygiene/HACCP-style system, traceability/recall, labelling, tax, land/access, insurance, and worker safety, with “check current local rules” banner.

### Figure 60.10 — EU/Irish 2026 Example
Jurisdiction-specific example: register before operating; food hygiene/traceability; honey-origin blend percentages under 2026 EU rules. Clearly label as an example rather than universal law.

### Figure 60.11 — Scaling Stages
Small manual apiary → dozens of colonies with dedicated transport/storage → hundreds with mechanisation/staff/wholesale systems. Show process redesign, not simple multiplication.

### Figure 60.12 — Risk Matrix
Weather, disease, supplier, customer concentration, equipment failure, cyber/data, legal compliance, and cash flow plotted by likelihood/impact.

### Figure 60.13 — Start-Up Sequence
Customer → small market test → legal map → budget/unit economics → registration/systems → controlled first batch → review → scale.

## Accuracy Requirements

- Do not present revenue as profit.
- Owner labour and colony replacement must be shown as real economic inputs.
- Any legal examples must be dated/jurisdiction-specific.
- Do not imply one hive-count threshold defines a commercial operation.
- Scaling visuals must show new system requirements rather than only more hives.
- Medical claims must not appear as legitimate marketing routes.

## Accessibility

- Use labels, shapes, patterns, and icons in addition to colour.
- Financial charts must remain readable in greyscale.
- Legal/compliance examples must use text disclaimers, not colour-only distinctions.
