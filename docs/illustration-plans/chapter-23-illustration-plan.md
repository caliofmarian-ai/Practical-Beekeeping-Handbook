# Chapter 23 — Beekeeping Safety and First Aid: Illustration Plan

Status: planned for final layout. No provisional artwork is embedded in the reader-facing manuscript.

## Planned Figures

### Figure 23.1 — Apiary Hazard Map
Plan-view apiary showing bees/flight paths, uneven ground, vehicle route, public access, livestock, hot smoker, treatment chemicals, lifting points and emergency-access path.

### Figure 23.2 — Simple Risk-Assessment Sequence
Hazard → who may be harmed → likelihood/severity → existing controls → additional controls → review. Use a generic matrix only as a concept, not a substitute for local workplace requirements.

### Figure 23.3 — Hierarchy of Controls for Beekeeping
Avoid/eliminate, substitute, engineering/site controls, procedures/administrative controls and PPE, with realistic apiary examples at each level.

### Figure 23.4 — Sting Prevention and Protective Overlap
Correct veil, cuffs, gloves, trouser/boot overlap and calm handling, with common gaps where bees can enter.

### Figure 23.5 — Honey-Bee Stinger Removal
Close-up showing a retained worker-bee stinger apparatus and the principle of prompt removal by scraping/brushing or grasping when that is faster. Caption: speed matters more than a single ideal technique.

### Figure 23.6 — Local Reaction Versus Emergency Warning Signs
Two-column clinical-awareness figure: limited local pain/swelling versus systemic warning signs such as breathing difficulty, widespread hives, collapse/dizziness or rapidly progressive swelling. Emergency panel directs the reader to emergency medical assistance and the person’s prescribed emergency plan.

### Figure 23.7 — Apiary First-Aid and Emergency Kit
Field kit showing first-aid supplies, charged phone, water, emergency contact/site directions, prescribed personal emergency medication where applicable and basic incident record card.

### Figure 23.8 — Safer Super Lifting
Stable stance, load close to body, mechanical/trolley aid and team lift contrasted with twisting, overreaching and unstable footing.

### Figure 23.9 — Heat, Cold and Dehydration Prevention
Hydration, shade/rest, layered weather protection and stop-work decision cues. Avoid presenting symptoms as a substitute for medical diagnosis.

### Figure 23.10 — Smoker and Vegetation-Fire Safety
Non-combustible resting area, spark control, fire restrictions, extinguisher/water where appropriate, and safe extinguishing/transport checks.

### Figure 23.11 — Chemical-Treatment PPE and Label Discipline
Generic authorised bee-treatment package with label, gloves/eye/respiratory PPE as specified, no food nearby, original container and wash-up/secure-storage pathway.

### Figure 23.12 — Lone-Working Emergency Plan
Apiary location shared with contact, expected return/check-in time, phone/signal plan, vehicle access and emergency-service location information.

## Accuracy Requirements

- First-aid artwork must not diagnose anaphylaxis from one isolated sign or imply home care replaces emergency medical assistance.
- Do not depict unprescribed medication use.
- Stinger-removal figure must emphasise prompt removal rather than promoting a disputed single technique.
- Chemical PPE must be label-dependent and product-generic; no off-label dosing or mixing.
- Lifting illustrations must show stable footing and realistic box mass/geometry.
- Safety controls must not rely on PPE alone.

## Accessibility and Layout

- Emergency warnings must use text/icon/shape, not red colour alone.
- Use high-contrast line art for first-aid sequences.
- Provide alt text describing the action and emergency decision point.
- Avoid graphic injury imagery that adds no instructional value.
