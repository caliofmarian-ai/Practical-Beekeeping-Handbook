# Illustration Plan — Chapter 34

## Chapter

Chapter 34 — Seasonal Beekeeping Calendar

## Purpose

This document defines the professional visual material to be produced during the final illustration and layout stage. No artwork from this plan is inserted into the manuscript at the drafting stage.

## Proposed Illustrations

### Illustration 34.1 — The Biological Colony Year

**Type:** Circular annual-cycle infographic

**Purpose:** Show the transition between winter survival, late-winter transition, spring buildup, swarm development, nectar flow, post-flow transition, late-season preparation, and winter entry.

**Required content:**

- eight clearly separated phases;
- directional annual movement;
- colony population curve;
- brood-production curve;
- forage-availability curve;
- indication that timing varies by region.

**Accuracy requirements:**

- curves must be presented as generalized patterns;
- the graphic must not imply universal calendar dates;
- population response must visibly lag behind brood expansion.

### Illustration 34.2 — Seasonal Decision Framework

**Type:** Professional decision-flow diagram

**Purpose:** Help the beekeeper identify the current colony phase.

**Decision points:**

- sustained flight or confinement;
- pollen intake;
- brood expansion;
- drone production;
- queen-cell development;
- active nectar storage;
- declining hive-weight gain;
- winter-bee production;
- brood contraction.

**Design requirement:** Use clean branching logic without excessive text.

### Illustration 34.3 — Northern and Southern Hemisphere Calendar Comparison

**Type:** Dual horizontal timeline

**Purpose:** Demonstrate that the biological sequence remains similar while calendar months shift.

**Required content:**

- Northern Hemisphere timing;
- Southern Hemisphere timing;
- note explaining that tropical regions may follow wet and dry seasons.

### Illustration 34.4 — Seasonal Inspection Objectives

**Type:** Six-panel field-reference plate

**Panels:**

1. winter external check;
2. first spring inspection;
3. swarm-period inspection;
4. nectar-flow space assessment;
5. post-flow health assessment;
6. autumn winter-readiness inspection.

**Accuracy requirements:**

- correct protective clothing;
- realistic hive components;
- minimal hive exposure in cold conditions;
- no unsafe handling.

### Illustration 34.5 — Population, Brood, Stores, and Parasite Pressure

**Type:** Multi-line seasonal scientific chart

**Purpose:** Explain why management must anticipate future conditions.

**Required content:**

- generalized adult-bee population;
- brood area;
- food consumption;
- stored food;
- parasite pressure;
- major management windows.

**Scientific caution:** All curves must be labelled as conceptual and locally variable.

### Illustration 34.6 — One-Phase-Ahead Planning

**Type:** Practical planning infographic

**Core questions:**

- current phase;
- next phase;
- colony resource needed;
- equipment needed;
- risk increasing;
- action that will soon become too late.

### Illustration 34.7 — Seasonal Equipment Readiness

**Type:** Organized equipment plate

**Groups:**

- winter;
- spring buildup;
- swarm period;
- harvest;
- autumn preparation.

**Purpose:** Show that equipment must be ready before the biological need occurs.

## Tables Recommended for Final Layout

- flexible seasonal calendar;
- month-by-month temperate calendar;
- Southern Hemisphere conversion table;
- seasonal equipment preparation matrix;
- seasonal inspection-purpose matrix.

## Visual Style

- scientifically accurate;
- publication quality;
- clean naturalistic rendering;
- consistent hive architecture throughout the handbook;
- neutral background;
- clear labels;
- accessible typography;
- no decorative elements that obscure technical information;
- no unsafe practices;
- no region-specific hive style unless explicitly identified.

## Final Production Checks

- confirm terminology against the final manuscript;
- confirm all seasonal curves are described as generalized;
- confirm Northern and Southern Hemisphere labels;
- confirm all equipment is realistic;
- confirm no artwork contradicts Chapters 14, 25–33, or 35;
- confirm captions explain local climatic variation;
- confirm numbering remains consistent with final layout.
