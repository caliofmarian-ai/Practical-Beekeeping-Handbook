# Chapter 45 — Viruses: Illustration Plan

Status: planned for final layout. No provisional artwork is embedded in the manuscript.

## Planned Figures

### Figure 45.1 — Infection Versus Clinical Disease
Layered diagram showing virus detected → covert/subclinical infection → overt disease, with modifiers including viral load, Varroa, bee age, nutrition, genetics and coinfection.

### Figure 45.2 — Viral Transmission Routes
Show horizontal food/contact transmission, vertical queen/offspring pathways, brood cannibalism and Varroa-mediated direct transmission.

### Figure 45.3 — Varroa–DWV Interaction
Brood-cell cross-section showing Varroa feeding during pupal development and direct virus transmission, followed by possible normal-looking or visibly deformed adult outcomes.

### Figure 45.4 — DWV Phenotype Spectrum
Panels: normal-looking infected bee, shortened abdomen, crumpled/deformed wings and severely impaired adult. Caption: visible deformity underestimates total infection burden.

### Figure 45.5 — CBPV Clinical Signs
Adult-bee panels showing trembling/crawling behaviour, hairless shiny appearance and clustered morbidity. Include a warning that abrasion, robbing and toxic exposure can mimic parts of the syndrome.

### Figure 45.6 — Acute Paralysis Virus Group
Conceptual relationship among ABPV, IAPV and KBV, with overlapping adult paralysis signs and requirement for molecular discrimination.

### Figure 45.7 — Sacbrood Development
Healthy larva beside infected larva showing raised head, sac-like skin and later dried remains. Include EFB/chilled-brood differential note.

### Figure 45.8 — Black Queen Cell Virus
Queen-cell cutaway showing an affected queen larva/pupa and darkened cell contents, clearly labelled as a possible syndrome rather than image-only proof.

### Figure 45.9 — RT-PCR Workflow
Fresh labelled sample → RNA extraction → reverse transcription → virus-specific amplification → result → interpretation with signs and sample type.

### Figure 45.10 — Pooled Versus Individual Sampling
Demonstrate how one highly infected moribund bee can be diluted in a pooled sample and why pooled surveillance and phenotype-targeted diagnosis answer different questions.

### Figure 45.11 — Viral Differential Diagnosis
Matrix for deformed wings, trembling adults, crawling bees, adult mortality and dead brood against Varroa-associated viruses, CBPV, acute viruses, poisoning, chilling, starvation and bacterial brood disease.

### Figure 45.12 — Practical Virus-Management Framework
Varroa control, nutrition, resistant stock, robbing reduction, movement biosecurity, exposure investigation and evidence-based laboratory testing. No direct antiviral product panel.

## Accuracy Requirements

- Do not show a positive PCR result as proof of causation.
- Varroa must be depicted as a major driver of DWV transmission and amplification.
- Do not imply all deformed wings are exclusively diagnostic of one virus.
- CBPV images must include differential-diagnosis caution.
- Do not depict unapproved pharmaceuticals as viral treatments.
- Molecular figures must distinguish RNA detection from proof of infectious virus.

## Accessibility

- Use labels, patterns and symbols in addition to colour.
- Ensure behavioural signs are described in captions for readers who cannot infer motion from still images.
- Keep molecular workflow understandable without specialist laboratory knowledge.
