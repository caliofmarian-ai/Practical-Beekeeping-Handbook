# Chapter 40 — Parasites: Illustration Plan

Status: planned for final layout. Illustration specifications remain outside the reader-facing chapter.

## Visual Objectives

The figures should enable accurate recognition of the major honey bee parasites, explain their different life cycles and transmission routes, and prevent the common error of treating every mite seen in a hive as Varroa.

## Planned Figures

### Figure 40.1 — Parasite, Pathogen, Pest, and Predator
- Type: classification infographic.
- Show representative examples of each category and the biological relationship to the honey bee.

### Figure 40.2 — Adult Female Varroa
- Type: labelled macro illustration.
- Show dorsal and ventral views, flattened oval body, legs and scale relative to an adult worker.
- Include a note that casual visual inspection is not an infestation estimate.

### Figure 40.3 — Varroa Reproductive Cycle
- Type: brood-cell sequence.
- Show foundress entry before capping, feeding, egg laying, offspring development, mating and emergence with the bee.
- Maintain biologically realistic brood stages and timing relationships without implying false fixed timing across all conditions.

### Figure 40.4 — Varroa on Adult Bees
- Type: anatomical illustration.
- Show protected positions between abdominal segments and feeding association with fat-body tissue.
- Avoid the outdated implication that mites merely ride on adult bees.

### Figure 40.5 — Direct and Indirect Varroa Damage
- Type: causal diagram.
- Varroa feeding → reduced host condition; Varroa-virus interaction → increased viral disease → shortened worker lifespan → colony population decline.

### Figure 40.6 — Varroa Spread Between Colonies
- Type: apiary network diagram.
- Show drifting, robbing, drones, swarms, brood transfer, packages/nuclei and beekeeper movement.

### Figure 40.7 — Varroa and Tropilaelaps Comparison
- Type: side-by-side macro illustration.
- Show relative shape: broad/flattened Varroa versus narrower, elongated Tropilaelaps.
- Include movement and brood-dependence notes in the caption rather than trying to show speed in a still image.

### Figure 40.8 — Tropilaelaps Life Cycle and Brood Dependence
- Type: brood-cell sequence.
- Show adult mite entry, reproduction on developing bee, emergence and rapid return to brood.
- Include a clearly labelled short adult-bee survival/dispersal interval without implying zero survival outside brood.

### Figure 40.9 — Tropilaelaps Surveillance Map Concept
- Type: dated distribution map for final publication.
- Map must be generated from authoritative data current at layout time, with an explicit “distribution changes” date.
- Do not freeze the 2026 distribution into future editions without rechecking.

### Figure 40.10 — Tracheal Mite Location
- Type: anatomical cutaway of adult worker thorax.
- Show prothoracic tracheae and microscopic *Acarapis woodi* within the tracheal lumen.

### Figure 40.11 — Tracheal Dissection Concept
- Type: diagnostic laboratory sequence.
- Show adult-bee sampling, thoracic preparation, pale normal trachea versus darkened/infested trachea, and microscopy.
- Make clear that “K-wing” is not diagnostic.

### Figure 40.12 — Not Every Hive Mite Is Varroa
- Type: identification caution plate.
- Show Varroa, Tropilaelaps and several generic non-parasitic/associated mites with strong labels that unusual specimens require competent identification.

### Figure 40.13 — Monitoring Method Matches the Question
- Type: decision diagram.
- Questions: estimate Varroa pressure; confirm treatment performance; detect exotic Tropilaelaps; investigate tracheal mites.
- Link each question to the appropriate class of method without embedding treatment thresholds that may change.

### Figure 40.14 — Parasite Biosecurity for Purchased Bees and Brood
- Type: flow diagram.
- Show traceable source → movement documentation → arrival inspection → baseline monitoring → segregation where appropriate → integration into routine records.

## Accuracy Requirements

- Use current taxonomy and scientific names.
- Do not depict Varroa as feeding only on haemolymph.
- Do not imply Tropilaelaps is confined to its historical Asian range; any distribution map must carry a date and authoritative source.
- Do not depict Tropilaelaps as unable to survive at all on adult bees; recent dispersal evidence must be respected.
- Do not use wing posture alone to diagnose tracheal mites.
- Relative mite sizes and body shapes must be realistic.
- Figures must not contain treatment recipes or fixed thresholds that could become obsolete.

## Accessibility and Layout

- Include scale bars where morphology is compared.
- Use labels and patterns in addition to colour.
- Use magnified inset panels for mites too small to see clearly at normal page scale.
- Captions must state what observation can and cannot establish.
