## Planned Figures

### Figure 42.1 — Nosema Life Cycle in an Adult Bee
Show ingestion of spores, germination, polar-tube extrusion, infection of a midgut epithelial cell, intracellular multiplication, new spore formation and faecal–oral transmission.

### Figure 42.2 — Nosema/Vairimorpha Naming
Simple taxonomy note showing the practical names *Nosema apis* and *Nosema ceranae* alongside proposed/current *Vairimorpha apis* and *Vairimorpha ceranae*. Caption must state that usage remains inconsistent across current literature.

### Figure 42.3 — Healthy Versus Heavily Affected Midgut Concept
Anatomical illustration of the adult digestive tract with the ventriculus/midgut highlighted. Avoid claiming that gross colour or appearance alone is diagnostic.

### Figure 42.4 — Dysentery Is Not a Diagnosis
Three hive-front panels: no staining, faecal staining associated with confinement/feed problems, and a Nosema-positive colony without obvious staining. Purpose: break the false one-sign/one-disease association.

### Figure 42.5 — Representative Sampling
Show older/returning workers, labelled collection container, colony ID, sample size specified by laboratory and separation of samples from different colonies.

### Figure 42.6 — Microscopy Workflow
Adult-bee sample → measured homogenisation → mixing → slide/counting chamber → microscope → Nosema-like spore detection.

### Figure 42.7 — Haemocytometer Counting Concept
Magnified counting grid showing spores and the variables required for a calculation: counted grid volume, dilution and number of bees. Do not embed one universal conversion constant.

### Figure 42.8 — Microscopy Versus PCR/qPCR
Comparison table: detects spores, species discrimination, quantification, equipment needs, sensitivity and main limitations.

### Figure 42.9 — Positive Result Does Not Equal Cause
Decision tree: positive test → sample quality → species → colony signs → Varroa/queen/nutrition/pesticide/other disease → management significance.

### Figure 42.10 — Faecal–Oral Transmission Routes
Show contaminated comb, cleaning behaviour, feeders/water, drifting, robbing and beekeeper movement of material.

### Figure 42.11 — Nosema Management Framework
Strong basic husbandry, food adequacy, moisture management, Varroa control, feeder hygiene, comb management, diagnosis, legal treatment check and follow-up.

### Figure 42.12 — Apiary-Level Pattern Chart
Example longitudinal chart comparing spore detection/species, colony strength, Varroa and season across several colonies. Emphasise trends over isolated counts.

## Accuracy Requirements

- Do not depict faecal staining as diagnostic proof.
- Do not imply that light microscopy reliably separates *N. apis* and *N. ceranae*.
- Do not include one universal spores-per-bee treatment threshold.
- Treatment artwork must remain jurisdiction-neutral and must not depict unauthorised medicine use.
- The polar tube and intracellular location should be biologically correct.
- Figures must distinguish pathogen detection from demonstrated clinical significance.

## Accessibility

- Use labels and shapes in addition to colour.
- Provide magnification cues for microscopic figures.
- Keep taxonomic nomenclature legible in print.
- Captions should explain both what the method shows and what it cannot prove.
