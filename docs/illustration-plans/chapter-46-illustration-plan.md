# Chapter 46 — Integrated Pest Management: Illustration Plan

Status: planned for final layout. No provisional artwork is embedded in the reader-facing manuscript.

## Planned Figures

### Figure 46.1 — The Honey Bee IPM Cycle
Circular decision diagram: prevention → surveillance/monitoring → identification → risk interpretation → control selection → authorised application → verification → records/review → back to prevention.

### Figure 46.2 — IPM Control Hierarchy
Layered pyramid showing prevention at the base, then cultural/genetic/mechanical/biological controls, selective authorised chemical controls, and verification across every level. Do not imply that chemistry is forbidden; show it as one evidence-based layer.

### Figure 46.3 — Surveillance Versus Monitoring
Two-column comparison: exotic-pest presence/absence surveillance versus repeated measurement of an established threat such as Varroa.

### Figure 46.4 — Thresholds Are Contextual
Decision wheel showing pest identity, season, brood level, climate, colony strength, virus pressure, management objective, future monitoring interval and local guidance. Add a separate statutory-control branch for notifiable diseases and exotic pests.

### Figure 46.5 — Brood Interruption as a Biological Window
Timeline showing normal capped-brood availability, a planned brood break, increased exposure of adult-associated mites, and a compatible authorised control window. Caption: timing and legal product directions remain essential.

### Figure 46.6 — Drone-Brood Removal Sequence
Dedicated drone comb → capping → mite reproduction → timely removal before emergence → hygienic handling → continued monitoring. Clearly show that late removal can increase mite production.

### Figure 46.7 — Robbing and Drift as Area-Wide Transmission
Apiary network showing mites/pathogens moving through robbing and drifting among nearby colonies, including a collapsing high-pressure source colony.

### Figure 46.8 — Genetic Resistance Within IPM
Show measurable traits such as Varroa-sensitive hygiene, grooming and suppressed mite reproduction feeding into lower pest pressure, while monitoring remains active.

### Figure 46.9 — Mode of Action and Resistance Selection
Population sequence showing susceptible versus resistant mites before and after repeated use of one mode of action. Include the difference between changing brand name and changing relevant mode of action.

### Figure 46.10 — Treatment Failure Investigation
Decision tree: high post-control result → verify sampling → review application/temperature/brood/product condition → assess resistance → assess reinvasion → choose evidence-based corrective action. Explicitly reject automatic dose escalation.

### Figure 46.11 — Food and Operator Safety Check
Pre-treatment panel: authorised product, honey-super status, withdrawal/residue requirements, temperature forecast, brood status, PPE, storage/disposal and follow-up test.

### Figure 46.12 — Established Pest Versus Exotic Regulated Pest
Two pathways: established Varroa uses monitoring/action interpretation; suspected exotic Tropilaelaps uses evidence preservation, movement restraint and competent-authority notification.

### Figure 46.13 — Annual IPM Plan
Season-neutral timeline with biological triggers rather than fixed months: brood expansion, flow, splits, migration, winter-bee preparation, post-treatment verification and season-end audit.

### Figure 46.14 — Apiary-Level Dashboard Concept
Colony rows with quantitative monitoring, queen line, brood status, intervention, active ingredient/method, verification result and next check. Purpose: show how records reveal recurring high-risk colonies and treatment performance.

## Accuracy Requirements

- Do not embed one universal Varroa threshold.
- Statutory disease and exotic-pest pathways must be clearly separated from ordinary economic-threshold logic.
- Do not portray “natural” controls as automatically safer than registered conventional products.
- Resistance figures must focus on active ingredient/mode of action rather than brand names.
- Combination treatments must not be shown unless explicitly authorised; the generic illustration should discourage unapproved mixing.
- Brood-break graphics must show both benefit and colony-production cost.
- Food-safety and operator-safety checks must precede chemical application.

## Accessibility

- Use icons, labels and patterns in addition to colour.
- Flow diagrams should remain understandable in greyscale.
- Avoid red/green-only safe/unsafe coding.
- Captions should explain the decision principle, not merely name the control.
