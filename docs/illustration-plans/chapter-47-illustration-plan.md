# Chapter 47 — Nectar Flow: Illustration Plan

Status: planned for final layout. No provisional artwork is embedded in the reader-facing manuscript.

## Planned Figures

### Figure 47.1 — From Flower to Harvestable Honey
Flow diagram separating plant nectar secretion → bee collection → colony intake → nectar ripening → stored surplus → harvestable honey. Emphasise that these quantities are related but not identical.

### Figure 47.2 — Floral Nectary and Nectar Collection
Botanically accurate flower cross-section showing nectary location, a foraging worker and nectar uptake. Use a generic flower plus optional crop-specific inset rather than implying all nectaries are identical.

### Figure 47.3 — Environmental Controls on Nectar Secretion
Decision-style infographic showing soil moisture, temperature, humidity, rain, wind, flower age and cultivar influencing nectar quantity/concentration.

### Figure 47.4 — Phenology Rather Than Calendar
Season-neutral sequence: bud → first bloom → partial bloom → peak bloom → declining bloom → end bloom, with space for local dates and hive-weight response.

### Figure 47.5 — Forager Recruitment and Receiver Bees
Colony diagram showing scouts, waggle-dance recruitment, returning foragers transferring nectar to receiver bees, temporary cell placement and ripening.

### Figure 47.6 — Nectar Ripening
Cell sequence showing fresh dilute nectar spread thinly, water loss and consolidation into mature honey. Avoid suggesting that capping alone is a perfect moisture test.

### Figure 47.7 — Typical Scale-Hive Daily Curve
Conceptual graph: morning forager departure dip, daytime nectar gain, evening maximum and overnight evaporative/metabolic loss. Caption: curves vary by colony and weather.

### Figure 47.8 — Scale-Hive Confounders
Examples of rain loading, super addition, swarm departure and equipment removal creating weight changes unrelated to nectar flow.

### Figure 47.9 — Colony Population Timing Before a Flow
Timeline connecting queen egg laying → brood development → young workers → forager population → anticipated peak bloom. Emphasise that workforce preparation begins weeks before bloom.

### Figure 47.10 — Storage-Space Decision
Strong-flow colony with insufficient space versus appropriate drawn-super capacity, showing brood-nest backfilling and congestion risk.

### Figure 47.11 — Flow, Dearth and Robbing Risk
Timeline showing major flow ending, colony switching from gain to consumption and increasing robbing pressure during dearth.

### Figure 47.12 — Local Bloom Record Sheet
Publication-ready form for plant, site, first/peak/end bloom, weather, bee visitation, scale response and reliability by year.

### Figure 47.13 — Varietal-Honey Source Separation
Conceptual timeline linking crop phenology, super placement/removal, scale data and traceability. State that mixed landscapes prevent perfect botanical separation in many cases.

## Accuracy Requirements

- Do not equate flower abundance with nectar secretion.
- Do not equate scale weight gain with finished honey weight.
- Seasonal examples must be phenological, not tied to one hemisphere’s fixed months.
- Nectar ripening artwork must reflect major water removal and internal handling.
- Floral-origin artwork must not imply that visual landscape observation alone proves botanical origin.

## Accessibility

- Use labels, icons and patterns in addition to colour.
- Graphs must remain interpretable in greyscale.
- Captions should explain the management inference and its limitations.
