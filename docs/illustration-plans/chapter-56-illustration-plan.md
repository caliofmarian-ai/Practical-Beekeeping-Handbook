### Figure 56.2 — Propolis Envelope and Colony Uses
Natural cavity cross-section showing resin coating on internal walls, crack sealing, entrance modification and encapsulation of an unremovable object.

Conceptual map with several chemically distinct propolis types (for example poplar-type, Brazilian green/red, Mediterranean/tropical types), explicitly showing that colour alone does not establish origin.

Clean flexible propolis trap compared with scraping from painted/dirty hive interfaces containing wood, paint and old wax. Emphasise source control.

Filled trap → controlled chilling/freezing → flexing/brittle release → cold sorting → labelled raw-propolis container, with condensation-control note.

### Figure 56.7 — Solvent Selectivity
Same raw propolis branching into ethanol, water and other suitable carrier extracts with different chemical profiles. Caption: extraction solvent changes composition and product stability.

### Figure 56.8 — Flammable-Solvent Safety
Professional ventilated ethanol-handling concept with closed compatible vessels, no ignition sources and compliant solvent storage. Do not show a home tincture recipe or open-flame heating.

### Figure 56.9 — Laboratory Characterisation Toolbox
HPLC/LC-MS, GC-MS, FTIR/NMR, total phenolics/flavonoids, residues/heavy metals and microbiology shown as complementary analyses.

### Figure 56.10 — Evidence Ladder
Raw composition → laboratory biological activity → animal research → human clinical evidence → authorised health/medicinal claim, showing that steps cannot be skipped.

### Figure 56.11 — Allergy Risk
Gloved worker handling propolis and a consumer warning concept for contact sensitivity; do not portray “natural” as allergen-free.

### Figure 56.12 — Propolis Traceability Chain
Apiary/hive materials and treatment history → trap/harvest lot → raw grade → extraction batch/solvent lot → laboratory results → final product/label.

### Figure 56.13 — Adulteration and Authentication
Excess wax, undeclared plant resin and fillers branching to chemical fingerprinting plus source traceability rather than colour/smell-only judgement.

## Accuracy Requirements

- Do not depict one global propolis formula or one colour as universal quality.
- Trap propolis must not be represented as automatically contaminant-free; it is cleaner by design but still requires controls.
- Solvent figures must show different extract profiles, not imply one solvent is universally superior.
- No figure should make therapeutic or disease-treatment claims.
- In-vitro antimicrobial/antioxidant activity must remain separate from clinical efficacy.
- Ethanol figures must prioritise fire/ventilation controls and omit DIY heating instructions.
- Allergy/contact-dermatitis risk must be visible in topical and occupational contexts.

## Accessibility

- Use patterns, icons and labels in addition to colour.
- Evidence ladder must be readable in greyscale.
- Chemical-analysis figures should use plain-language captions alongside technical abbreviations.
