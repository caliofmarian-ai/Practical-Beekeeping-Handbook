# Chapter 43 — American Foulbrood: Illustration Plan

Status: planned for final layout. No provisional artwork is included in the reader-facing chapter.

## Planned Figures

### Figure 43.1 — AFB Infection Cycle
Show contaminated food containing spores → ingestion by a young larva → spore germination → vegetative bacterial multiplication → larval death → decomposition → adherent scale containing persistent spores → worker-mediated redistribution.

### Figure 43.2 — Healthy Brood Versus Early AFB Suspicion
High-detail comb panels comparing healthy sealed brood with irregular cappings, sunken cells and small perforations. Caption: pattern supports investigation but does not prove AFB.

### Figure 43.3 — Disease-Stage Cell Series
Cross-sections showing normal larva, discoloured remains, ropey stage and dry adherent scale. Use biologically realistic positions and clearly mark that not every case passes through an easily observed ropey stage.

### Figure 43.4 — Rope Test
Numbered visual using a disposable/cleanable probe, showing suitable suspect material and immediate containment of the contaminated probe. Caption must state that the field test is supporting evidence, not universal definitive confirmation.

### Figure 43.5 — Adherent Scale and Pupal Tongue
Magnified cell views showing typical scale position and an optional pupal-tongue example. Caption: absence of either sign does not exclude disease.

### Figure 43.6 — AFB Differential Diagnosis
Comparison panels for AFB, EFB, sacbrood, chilled brood and Varroa-associated brood removal. Focus on stage, larval position, capping and remains without implying image-only diagnosis.

### Figure 43.7 — Routes of Spore Movement
Apiary network showing robbing, drifting, brood/honey frame transfer, contaminated tools, second-hand comb, feeders, extraction equipment and moved colonies.

### Figure 43.8 — Suspect-Colony Containment
Plan view: clearly marked suspect hive, reduced unnecessary access, dedicated tools, no frame movement, protected honey, labelled sample and contact with competent authority.

### Figure 43.9 — Laboratory Confirmation Pathways
Sample → culture/immunological/PCR/qPCR or validated current method → diagnostic interpretation → regulatory decision. Keep methods generic enough for international use.

### Figure 43.10 — Cleaning Versus Decontamination
Two-stage technical graphic showing physical removal of wax/propolis/debris followed by an approved spore-targeting process. Do not embed chemical recipes.

### Figure 43.11 — Why Antibiotics Do Not Eradicate AFB
Diagram separating vegetative bacteria from durable spores: antimicrobial suppression may reduce active growth while spores persist on comb.

### Figure 43.12 — Trace-Back and Trace-Forward Map
Show source purchases, brood-frame transfers, apiary movements, dead-outs, colony sales and shared equipment to illustrate outbreak tracing.

### Figure 43.13 — AFB Response Decision Flow
Suspicious signs → stop movement → record/preserve evidence → contact authority where required → approved confirmation → official control/decontamination → inspection of contact colonies → follow-up surveillance.

## Accuracy Requirements

- Spore persistence must be visually central.
- Do not depict the rope test, odour or one brood sign as conclusive proof.
- Never show unauthorised destruction, burning or chemical decontamination recipes.
- Antibiotic artwork must explicitly show survival of spores.
- Any legal-control symbol must state that requirements vary by jurisdiction.
- Experimental bacteriophage research may appear only as a clearly labelled research/future-tools inset, not established universal field control.

## Accessibility

- Use labels, cell outlines and patterns in addition to colour.
- Ensure disease-stage panels remain readable in greyscale.
- Provide magnified cell insets and scale cues.
- Captions should state both the useful observation and its diagnostic limitation.
