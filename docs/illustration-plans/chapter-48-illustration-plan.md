## Planned Figures

### Figure 48.1 — Harvest Readiness Decision Flow
Crop maturity → representative moisture → treatment status → brood check → colony-store check → weather/logistics → harvest or delay/segregate.

### Figure 48.2 — Capped, Uncapped, and Fresh Nectar
Realistic frame panels showing capped mature honey, uncapped but potentially mature honey, and very dilute fresh nectar. Caption: appearance alone does not quantify moisture.

### Figure 48.3 — Honey Refractometer Use
Numbered workflow: calibration/check → clean dry prism → representative sample → equilibrate/read → record → clean between samples. Provide optical and digital insets.

### Figure 48.4 — Representative Moisture Sampling
Super diagram showing sample positions across central/outer frames and capped/uncapped areas, illustrating why one drop from one cell can misrepresent a lot.

### Figure 48.5 — Legal Maximum Versus Operational Target
Conceptual chart showing a jurisdictional maximum as a compliance boundary and a lower producer specification as a larger storage-safety margin. Do not hard-code one global operational target.

### Figure 48.6 — Fermentation Risk Factors
Moisture, yeast load, temperature, time and contamination feeding into increasing fermentation risk. Emphasise continuous rather than one-step risk.

### Figure 48.7 — Bee-Removal Methods Compared
Bee escape, brush/shake and blower panels with advantages, limitations, scale suitability and contamination cautions. Chemical repellents shown only as “authorised product where permitted”.

### Figure 48.8 — Robbing-Safe Harvest Workflow
Open hive → bee removal → immediate super cover → clean transport → closed honey room, with spilled honey shown as a risk path to avoid.

### Figure 48.9 — Safe Manual Handling of Supers
Stable lift, load close to body, no twisting, clear walking route, trolley/mechanical aid and team lift for awkward loads.

### Figure 48.10 — Food-Safe Transport
Covered food supers on clean pallets/liners inside a clean dry vehicle, separated from fuel/chemicals and secured against load shift.

### Figure 48.11 — Traceability Chain
Apiary/colony → harvest date → super/lot ID → moisture/treatment record → extraction batch → storage tank → final pack lot.

### Figure 48.12 — EU Blend-Origin Traceability Context
High-level 2026 EU example: multiple country origins must remain traceable to percentage shares used for labelling. Explicitly label as jurisdiction-specific and time-dated.

### Figure 48.13 — Harvest Emergency Responses
Panels for rain-wetted supers, broken comb leak, chemical taint and brood found in harvest comb, each showing immediate segregation rather than blending.

## Accuracy Requirements

- Do not claim that capped cells are always below a specific moisture percentage.
- Do not depict a legal 20% maximum as the recommended universal harvest target.
- Calibration medium must be instrument-specific.
- ATC must not be portrayed as correcting poor sampling or wet prisms.
- Brood must be excluded from ordinary extraction visuals.
- Food supers must never be shown on soil or dirty vehicle surfaces.
- EU origin-labelling figure must be dated to the rules applying from 14 June 2026.

## Accessibility

- Use labels and symbols in addition to colour.
- Moisture/compliance charts must work in greyscale.
- Captions should distinguish regulatory examples from universal biological principles.
