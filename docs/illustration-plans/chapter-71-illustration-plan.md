Direct toxicity plus indirect loss of flowering plants, drift and mixture exposure. Avoid implying identical risk for all products.

Low, moderate and high managed-colony density over the same flowering landscape, showing increasing resource demand and drifting/pathogen opportunities without claiming a universal threshold.

### Figure 71.9 — Competition Is Context Dependent
Decision wheel: floral abundance, season, wild species, drought, colony density, spatial scale and habitat quality.

### Figure 71.10 — Biodiversity Monitoring Toolkit
Habitat map, flower transect, pollinator transect, fixed photographs, nesting-resource count, citizen-science record and optional molecular tools.

### Figure 71.11 — Conservation Action Hierarchy
Protect existing high-quality habitat → improve management → connect patches → restore missing resources → plant/create habitat → add managed hives only where justified for a separate beekeeping/pollination goal.

## Accuracy Requirements

- Managed honey-bee numbers must never be used as a stand-alone biodiversity metric.
- Competition figures must communicate uncertainty/context rather than a universal harmful/harmless conclusion.
- Bee hotels must not be presented as superior to natural nesting habitat.
- Invasive nectar plants must not be promoted simply for bee visitation.
- AIPP is a voluntary Irish framework, not statutory law.
- Climate/mismatch figures should avoid deterministic claims where current evidence is mixed.

## Accessibility

- Use labels, icons and textures in addition to colour.
- Habitat maps and seasonal calendars must work in greyscale.
- Pollinator groups should be distinguishable by shape as well as colour.
