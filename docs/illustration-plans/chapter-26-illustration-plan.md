# Chapter 26 — Feeding Bees: Illustration Plan

## Status

Planned for the final illustration and layout stage.

## Purpose

The visual material should help readers select, prepare and deliver supplemental feed safely without duplicating the written explanations.

## Visual 1 — Natural and Supplemental Nutrition

### Format

Educational biological illustration.

### Content

Show a colony's principal nutritional inputs:

- Nectar and honey as carbohydrate sources.
- pollen and bee bread as protein, lipid, vitamin and mineral sources.
- water for dilution, brood-food preparation and cooling.
- supplemental syrup and protein feed as temporary management tools.

### Required qualities

- Scientifically accurate worker-bee anatomy and hive context.
- Clear distinction between natural forage and supplemental feed.
- No commercial branding.
- No implication that supplementation replaces natural forage.

## Visual 2 — Syrup Preparation by Weight

### Format

Step-by-step instructional sequence.

### Content

Show:

1. Clean food-grade equipment.
2. measured water.
3. controlled warming.
4. heat switched off.
5. measured white granulated sugar added.
6. stirring until dissolved.
7. complete cooling.
8. labelled sealed container.

Include separate examples for:

- light syrup: one part sugar to one part water by weight;
- heavy syrup: two parts sugar to one part water by weight.

### Safety details

- No boiling syrup.
- No hot feed placed in a hive.
- No darkened or caramelised syrup.
- Stable vessel and protected hands.

## Visual 3 — Feeder Types

### Format

Comparative cutaway plate.

### Content

Show:

- Entrance feeder.
- top feeder.
- frame feeder.
- pail feeder.
- bag feeder.

For each feeder, indicate:

- Colony access.
- syrup reservoir.
- anti-drowning feature.
- likely leakage point.
- relative robbing exposure.

### Composition

All feeder examples should use the same hive scale and viewing angle where practical.

## Visual 4 — Anti-Drowning Features

### Format

Detailed technical illustration.

### Content

Compare:

- Float.
- ladder.
- mesh access.
- roughened wall.
- restricted access channel.

Show bees standing safely above the syrup surface.

## Visual 5 — Robbing Prevention During Feeding

### Format

Before-and-after apiary scene.

### Unsafe side

Show:

- Spilled syrup.
- exposed comb.
- wide entrance on a weak colony.
- leaking external feeder.
- bees searching hive joints.
- sticky open containers.

### Safe side

Show:

- Internal feeder.
- clean ground and equipment.
- reduced defensible entrance.
- fully closed hive.
- sealed transport container.
- prompt spill cleanup.

## Visual 6 — Brood Nest Before and After Overfeeding

### Format

Two brood-frame diagrams rendered as realistic comb illustrations.

### Balanced frame

Show:

- Central brood.
- pollen band.
- peripheral food.
- empty polished cells available for laying.

### Syrup-bound frame

Show:

- Syrup occupying central brood cells.
- reduced laying space.
- compressed brood area.

### Accuracy requirement

Use realistic worker-cell proportions and avoid exaggerated geometric patterns.

## Visual 7 — Feeding Decision Flow

### Format

Professional decision chart.

### Decision sequence

1. Does the colony have a defined feeding need?
2. Is it queenright and otherwise healthy?
3. Is carbohydrate, protein or another management correction required?
4. Are harvestable honey supers absent?
5. Can the colony defend and access an internal feeder?
6. What quantity and concentration match the purpose?
7. Is the colony responding without robbing or brood restriction?
8. Has the objective been achieved?
9. Stop feeding and remove the feeder.

### Design requirement

Use clean publication typography and standard decision symbols rather than ASCII artwork.

## Visual 8 — Protein Patty Placement

### Format

Hive cross-section.

### Content

Show a correctly sized protein patty positioned above brood where nurse bees can reach it.

Also show:

- Proper bee space.
- small portion size.
- inspection for mould.
- removal of uneaten material.
- small hive beetle risk in neglected feed.

## Visual 9 — Feeding Record Example

### Format

Clean specimen record sheet.

### Fields

- Apiary.
- colony.
- date.
- feeding purpose.
- feed type.
- concentration.
- quantity.
- feeder.
- remaining quantity.
- forage conditions.
- colony response.
- feeding end date.
- harvest-super installation date.

Use fictional colony identifiers and no private information.

## Visual 10 — Honey-Quality Safeguard

### Format

Separated process illustration.

### Content

Show:

- Feeding phase with no harvest supers.
- feeding stopped.
- feeder removed and records completed.
- colony reassessed.
- harvest supers installed only after separation is confirmed.

### Message

Supplemental sugar feed must not be stored in comb intended for sale as honey.

## Style Direction

- Professional handbook illustration.
- Natural honey bee colours.
- Accurate hive components.
- Clear visual hierarchy.
- Legible labels.
- Consistent perspective.
- No cartoon anthropomorphism.
- No manufacturer logos.
- No decorative imagery that obscures instruction.
- Suitable for both print and digital publication.

## Production Notes

Illustrations should be produced together with the full-book visual system during final layout so that line weight, typography, dimensions, captions and colour treatment remain consistent across all chapters.
