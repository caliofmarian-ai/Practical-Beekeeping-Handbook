# Chapter 38 — Emergency Colony Problems: Illustration Plan

Status: planned for final layout; no provisional artwork is to be inserted into the reader-facing manuscript.

## Objectives

The visual set should help a beekeeper recognise emergencies quickly, choose the safest first action, and distinguish stabilisation from diagnosis. Artwork must follow `docs/standards/ILLUSTRATION_STANDARDS.md` and remain scientifically and mechanically accurate.

## Planned Visuals

### Figure 38.1 — Emergency Triage Sequence
- Type: decision-flow diagram.
- Show: people/public safety → stop active hazard → stabilise colony → prevent spread → preserve evidence → diagnose → correct cause → verify recovery.
- Emphasise that regulatory contact can interrupt the flow whenever a notifiable disease or chemical incident is suspected.

### Figure 38.2 — Starvation Versus Accessible Stores
- Type: cutaway hive comparison.
- Panel A: cluster in contact with food.
- Panel B: small cold cluster separated from remaining food.
- Labels: cluster, empty cells, remaining stores, safe placement zone for emergency feed.

### Figure 38.3 — Severe Robbing at the Entrance
- Type: behavioural illustration.
- Show fighting, bees seeking cracks, wax debris, irregular flight, reduced entrance, and exposed syrup as a preventable trigger.
- Avoid depicting normal orientation flights as robbing.

### Figure 38.4 — Transport Overheating
- Type: transport safety diagram.
- Show adequate versus inadequate ventilation, solar load, clustering against mesh, secure straps, and airflow around colonies.
- Include a clear warning against sealing a populous colony without ventilation.

### Figure 38.5 — Comb Collapse and Frame Handling
- Type: handling sequence.
- Show a softened natural comb, correct vertical frame orientation, support/repair concept, and incorrect tilting.

### Figure 38.6 — Storm-Damaged Hive Recovery
- Type: numbered reconstruction sequence.
- Show displaced roof/boxes, exposed frames, reassembly in original order, straps, weatherproofing, and removal of spilled honey.

### Figure 38.7 — Sudden Mortality Evidence Map
- Type: diagnostic observation diagram.
- Compare dead bees concentrated at the entrance, bees head-first in empty cells, transport mortality, and scattered crawling bees.
- Purpose: demonstrate that distribution is evidence, not a diagnosis.

### Figure 38.8 — Suspected Disease Biosecurity Zone
- Type: apiary layout.
- Show clean-to-dirty work order, separate tools, no frame transfer, sample labelling, and restricted movement from the suspect colony.

### Figure 38.9 — Emergency Record Sheet Example
- Type: publication-ready form.
- Fields: apiary/colony ID, date/time, weather, objective observations, photographs, adult population, brood, food, treatments, feeding, samples, contacts, actions, follow-up, outcome.

### Figure 38.10 — Recovery Monitoring Timeline
- Type: simple timeline.
- Show immediate stabilisation, short follow-up, biological response interval, and final verification.
- Note that appropriate interval depends on the problem and colony biology.

## Accuracy Requirements

- Do not portray one visual sign as diagnostic proof.
- Do not depict illegal/off-label treatment.
- Use generic hive designs where the principle is universal.
- Maintain realistic bee-space, frame orientation, and transport geometry.
- Safety labels must appear before hazardous actions.
- Food-contamination visuals must clearly separate marketable from quarantined product.

## Final-Layout Notes

- Prefer vector line art for decision diagrams and technical procedures.
- Use restrained colour coding that remains legible in greyscale.
- Do not rely on red/green alone for safe/unsafe states.
- Captions should explicitly state the decision lesson, not merely name the picture.
