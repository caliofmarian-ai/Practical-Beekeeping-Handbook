# Chapter 39 — Diseases: Illustration Plan

Status: planned for final layout. No provisional artwork belongs in the reader-facing manuscript.

## Visual Objectives

The figures should teach pattern recognition while making clear that visual signs support investigation but do not automatically prove a diagnosis.

## Planned Figures

### Figure 39.1 — Disease Investigation Framework
History → external observation → adult and brood assessment → differential diagnosis → sampling/confirmation → control → follow-up. Include a branch to the competent authority when regulated disease is suspected.

### Figure 39.2 — Healthy Brood Reference
Label eggs, healthy pearly larvae of several ages, normally capped worker brood, pollen and food cells. This provides the normal comparison for later figures.

### Figure 39.3 — Patchy Brood Has Multiple Causes
Show conceptually similar patchiness associated with queen problems, infectious brood disease, hygienic removal, brood chilling and parasite-associated brood loss. Caption: appearance alone is not diagnostic.

### Figure 39.4 — American Foulbrood Suspicion Features
Detailed brood-comb and cell views showing sunken or perforated cappings, discoloured remains and adherent scale position. If a rope-test panel is included, explicitly label it as supporting evidence rather than definitive confirmation.

### Figure 39.5 — European Foulbrood Suspicion Features
Open-brood close-up comparing normal larvae with discoloured, twisted or collapsed affected larvae. Include age context and a note that confirmation may be required.

### Figure 39.6 — AFB and EFB Comparison
Infographic comparing usual affected stage, persistence, common field observations, transmission concerns and confirmation requirements.

### Figure 39.7 — Chalkbrood Progression
Show representative white, grey/mottled and dark mummies in cells and after worker removal.

### Figure 39.8 — Sacbrood Larva
Show a healthy larva beside a representative sacbrood-affected larva with characteristic raised head and sac-like form.

### Figure 39.9 — Adult-Bee Warning Signs
Four panels: normal worker, deformed-wing phenotype, trembling/crawling signs and hive-front faecal spotting. Caption: each is a sign with multiple possible causes.

### Figure 39.10 — Correct Sampling and Labelling
Show clean colony-specific container, sample label, colony ID, date, clean tools/gloves, record sheet and separate packaging for separate colonies.

### Figure 39.11 — Apiary Biosecurity Flow
Plan view showing healthy colonies first, suspect colony last, dedicated/cleaned tools, no frame exchange, control of exposed feed and segregation of suspect equipment.

### Figure 39.12 — Routes of Disease Spread
Network diagram showing drifting, robbing, introduced bees, brood transfer, tools, used comb/equipment and shared processing equipment.

### Figure 39.13 — Differential Diagnosis Matrix
Rows: patchy brood, dead larvae, crawling adults, deformed wings, faecal spotting and sudden mortality. Columns: infectious disease, parasites, queen problems, nutrition, temperature and environmental exposure. Use “possible” indicators rather than diagnostic labels.

### Figure 39.14 — Disease Event Record and Apiary Map
Example record sheet plus simplified apiary map showing how colony identity, dates and location can reveal spatial and temporal patterns.

## Accuracy Requirements

- Disease signs must be anatomically and developmentally realistic.
- Do not present one visual feature as conclusive diagnosis.
- AFB persistence must be differentiated accurately from EFB biology without implying that EFB is unimportant.
- Where *Nosema*/*Vairimorpha* nomenclature appears, note the current practical naming issue.
- Do not imply that all deformed wings prove one specific viral diagnosis.
- Any regulatory symbol must say to check current local requirements.

## Accessibility and Layout

- Use labels, shapes and patterns in addition to colour.
- Keep cell details legible in print.
- Use consistent magnification indicators.
- Captions must explain the diagnostic lesson, not merely name the picture.
