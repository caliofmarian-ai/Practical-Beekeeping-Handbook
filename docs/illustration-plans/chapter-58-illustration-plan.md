## Planned Figures

### Figure 58.1 — Nurse-Bee Glands and Royal Jelly Production
Anatomically accurate worker head showing hypopharyngeal and mandibular glands and brood-food secretion, with queen and worker larvae as feeding destinations.

### Figure 58.2 — Queen Versus Worker Feeding
Parallel developmental concept showing continuous abundant royal-jelly provisioning for queen-destined larvae versus changing worker-brood diet. Avoid implying royal jelly is a single “queen hormone”.

### Figure 58.3 — Commercial Production Cycle
Strong nurse-rich colony → queen cups with very young larvae → cell acceptance/provisioning → harvest before advanced larval consumption → larva removal → clean royal-jelly collection → immediate cooling.

### Figure 58.4 — Nurse Capacity Versus Cell Load
Balanced number of accepted cells with high jelly volume compared with excessive cell load producing lower average provisioning.

### Figure 58.5 — Safe Grafting
Very young larva, clean grafting tool, shaded work area, minimal out-of-hive exposure, and rapid return to the cell builder.

### Figure 58.6 — Hygienic Harvest
Queen-cell strip → larva removal → clean spatula/suction collection → food-compatible labelled container → chilled storage. Keep brood debris visibly separate.

### Figure 58.7 — Cold Chain
Apiary harvest → rapid first cooling → refrigerated/frozen transport → cold processing → cold storage/distribution, with temperature logging at transitions.

### Figure 58.8 — Royal Jelly Quality Dimensions
Sensory quality, moisture, protein/MRJP profile, sugars, 10-HDA, acidity, volatiles, microbiology, residues, and traceability shown as complementary evidence.

### Figure 58.9 — Why 10-HDA Alone Is Not a Freshness Test
Stable 10-HDA line beside changing protein/MRJP, volatile, enzyme, and Maillard markers during poor storage. Caption: identity marker does not equal complete freshness marker.

### Figure 58.10 — Storage and Freshness Risks
Heat, light, oxygen, time, repeated thawing, and poor hygiene leading to protein denaturation, oxidation, volatile loss, colour/viscosity change, and contamination.

### Figure 58.11 — Product Forms
Fresh chilled royal jelly, frozen royal jelly, freeze-dried powder, and formulated product, each with distinct packaging and storage requirements.

### Figure 58.12 — Adulteration Investigation
Water/syrup/milk-derived/filler adulteration possibilities leading to multi-parameter laboratory analysis: sugars, lactose, 10-HDA, proteins, minerals, spectroscopy/LC methods, and traceability.

### Figure 58.13 — Allergy and Claims Boundary
Allergy/anaphylaxis warning beside evidence ladder from laboratory research to clinical evidence to authorised health/medicinal claims.

### Figure 58.14 — Current Standards
ISO 12824:2016 for royal-jelly specifications and ISO 24364:2023 for production, both clearly shown as current reference frameworks in 2026.

## Accuracy Requirements

- Gland anatomy and larval feeding must be biologically accurate.
- Do not imply royal jelly is honey or that one compound causes all queen development.
- No rigid universal graft/harvest hour recipe should be embedded.
- Cold-chain figures must show prompt first cooling rather than only warehouse refrigeration.
- 10-HDA must not be portrayed as a complete freshness indicator.
- Allergy/anaphylaxis risk must be visible.
- Laboratory biological activity must not be converted into therapeutic claims.
- Product forms must show that drying/formulation changes quality and labelling requirements.

## Accessibility

- Use labels, patterns, and symbols in addition to colour.
- Developmental timelines must remain clear in greyscale.
- Safety/claims graphics must use text cues, not colour alone.
- Molecular-quality figures should include plain-language captions.
