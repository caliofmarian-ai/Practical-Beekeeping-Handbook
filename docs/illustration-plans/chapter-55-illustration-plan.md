## Planned Figures

### Figure 55.1 — Wax Production by a Worker Bee
Anatomically accurate worker underside showing abdominal wax glands, emerging wax scales, transfer by legs and manipulation at the mandibles.

### Figure 55.2 — Wax Source Streams
Cappings wax, light honey-super comb, old brood comb and contaminated/disease-suspect material shown as separate streams with different quality and destination decisions.

### Figure 55.3 — New Comb Versus Old Brood Comb
Cross-section showing clean wax cells compared with repeated cocoon layers, propolis and brood debris. Include decreasing recoverable-wax fraction conceptually.

### Figure 55.4 — Lipophilic Residue Accumulation
Conceptual diagram showing repeated hive treatments/environmental exposures partitioning into long-lived wax and then potentially entering recycled foundation. Do not imply every residue behaves identically.

### Figure 55.5 — Rendering Methods Compared
Solar melter, water rendering and purpose-built steam melter with advantages, limitations and principal hazards. Do not depict improvised sealed pressure vessels or open-flame pans.

### Figure 55.6 — Water Rendering Separation
Hot wax/water/debris mixture settling into floating wax, water and dense slumgum layers, followed by solidified wax cake and dirty underside removal.

### Figure 55.7 — Safe Molten-Wax Workstation
Stable indirect heat, temperature measurement, appropriate PPE, ventilation, clear floor, lid/fire-control plan and no children/animals in the work zone.

### Figure 55.8 — Wax Fire and Water Hazard
Safety illustration showing that water added to overheated/burning molten wax can spread/splash the fuel. Direct reader to trained fire response/local fire guidance rather than a DIY extinguishing recipe.

### Figure 55.9 — Coarse and Fine Physical Filtration
Two-stage hot-wax filtration removing physical debris, with a separate note that dissolved lipophilic residues/adulterants remain a chemical-quality question.

### Figure 55.10 — Wax Block Cooling and Shrinkage
Clean mould fill → cooling contraction → normal central depression → underside debris inspection → labelled finished block.

### Figure 55.11 — End-Use Quality Ladder
Foundation, candle/craft, cosmetic and regulated food-additive uses with increasingly specific traceability/testing requirements; avoid implying one strict universal ranking because specifications differ by market.

### Figure 55.12 — Beeswax Adulteration
Authentic wax mixed conceptually with paraffin, microcrystalline wax or stearin, followed by laboratory methods FTIR, DSC, chromatography and classical indices. Cross out appearance/melting point as stand-alone proof.

### Figure 55.13 — EU E901 Context
Apiary-rendered wax → purification/testing/specification compliance → regulated E901 supply chain. Clearly label this as an EU jurisdiction-specific example and state that biological origin alone is insufficient.

### Figure 55.14 — Wax Lot Traceability
Apiary/source stream → treatment/disease history → rendering batch → block IDs → laboratory results/end use → buyer or internal foundation batch.

## Accuracy Requirements

- Wax glands and scale location must be anatomically correct.
- Do not show all dark wax as chemically contaminated; darkening has multiple biological causes.
- Do not imply rendering removes lipophilic residues or guarantees destruction of AFB spores.
- No illustration may show an unattended open-flame wax pot.
- Steam equipment must be visibly vented/purpose-built; no improvised pressure-vessel design.
- Do not hard-code one melting point as proof of authenticity.
- E901 must be clearly presented as a specification-controlled material, not any block of rendered wax.

## Accessibility

- Use labels, texture and patterns in addition to colour.
- Safety diagrams must not rely on red/green alone.
- Analytical-method panels should use plain-language captions explaining what each method contributes.
