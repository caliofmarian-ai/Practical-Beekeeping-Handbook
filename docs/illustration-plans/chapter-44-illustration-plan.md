# Chapter 44 — European Foulbrood: Illustration Plan

Status: planned for final layout. No provisional artwork is embedded in the manuscript.

## Planned Figures

### Figure 44.1 — EFB Infection Sequence
Young larva receives contaminated food → *Melissococcus plutonius* multiplies in the gut → larval nutrition and growth are disrupted → discolouration/abnormal position → worker removal or larval death → contamination persists in colony material.

### Figure 44.2 — Healthy Versus EFB-Affected Open Brood
Detailed cells comparing pearly C-shaped larvae with creamy/yellow/brown twisted or collapsed larvae. Include larvae of comparable age.

### Figure 44.3 — Abnormal Larval Positions
Several realistic examples: twisted, lengthwise, flattened and collapsed larvae. Caption: position supports suspicion but is not diagnostic alone.

### Figure 44.4 — EFB Versus AFB
Side-by-side comparison of usual brood stage, larval position, cappings, remains, spore biology and typical scale adhesion. Avoid absolute claims where presentation overlaps.

### Figure 44.5 — EFB Differential Diagnosis
Panels for EFB, chilled brood, sacbrood, queen-pattern failure, nutritional brood loss and Varroa-associated brood removal.

### Figure 44.6 — Disease Expression and Colony Stress
Conceptual diagram showing *M. plutonius* infection as the infectious cause while nurse-bee number, forage interruption, weather and nutrition modify clinical expression.

### Figure 44.7 — EFB Transmission Routes
Show brood/food-frame transfer, contaminated comb, robbing, drifting, tools, feeders and moved colonies.

### Figure 44.8 — Sampling and Diagnostic Pathway
Representative larva → labelled sample → validated field test/laboratory test → interpretation with clinical signs and legal context.

### Figure 44.9 — Shook-Swarm Principle
High-level official-control concept only: adult bees separated from contaminated brood comb and installed on clean equipment under an approved programme. Do not include unauthorised step-by-step operational parameters.

### Figure 44.10 — Follow-Up After Apparent Recovery
Timeline showing improved forage/clinical appearance, mandatory reinspection, colony-strength check and recurrence surveillance.

### Figure 44.11 — Apiary Contact Map
Illustrate frame transfers, shared equipment, robbing and introduced colonies around an index EFB case.

## Accuracy Requirements

- Do not depict poor nutrition as the infectious cause of EFB.
- Do not imply EFB forms AFB-like bacterial endospores.
- Do not present smell, visible tracheae or one larval position as diagnostic proof.
- Treatment graphics must remain jurisdiction-neutral.
- Antibiotics must never be illustrated as routine or universally authorised.
- Any shook-swarm or destruction visual must be clearly labelled as subject to competent-authority requirements.

## Accessibility

- Use labels, shape and texture in addition to colour.
- Ensure larval details remain legible in print.
- Captions must distinguish suggestive signs from confirmed diagnosis.
