Anatomically accurate abdomen cutaway showing venom gland, venom reservoir, sting shaft/lancets, and delivery path. Include a small queen-sting comparison without implying queens are used commercially.

Conceptual composition wheel showing melittin, phospholipase A2, apamin, hyaluronidase, mast-cell-degranulating peptide, and minor components. Avoid fixed universal percentages.

Local reaction → large local reaction → systemic allergy/anaphylaxis, with emergency warning signs and direction to emergency medical care.

Hive-collected raw venom on one side; standardised medicinal allergen preparation administered by allergy specialists on the other. Strong visual boundary: not interchangeable.

### Figure 59.5 — Professional Electrical Collection Principle
Purpose-built commercial collector with guarded stimulation grid and glass/membrane collection surface, bees depositing venom and surviving where the system permits. No circuit diagram, voltage recipe, exposed conductor design, or DIY wiring.

### Figure 59.6 — Safe Collection Site
Controlled apiary with exclusion zone, PPE, warning/communication, livestock/public separation, emergency vehicle access, and no collection beside public paths.

### Figure 59.7 — Pre-Use Equipment Inspection
Commercial collector, insulation, leads, battery/charger, housing, plate, membrane, and dry-condition checks; damaged equipment placed out of service.

### Figure 59.8 — Dry Venom Recovery
Clean collection plate → low-dust scraping with eye/skin protection → closed labelled vial → controlled cold/dry storage. Include glass-fragment/foreign-body control.

### Figure 59.9 — Occupational Exposure Routes
Stings, skin contact, eye contact, and inhalation of dry particles, each linked to appropriate prevention controls.

### Figure 59.10 — Multicomponent Quality Control
Melittin, PLA2 content/activity, apamin, moisture, peptide/protein profile, microbiology, contaminants, and traceability. Caption: melittin alone is insufficient.

### Figure 59.11 — Melittin-Equivalence Concept
Two batches with nearly equal melittin but different PLA2, moisture, and wider profiles, illustrating the 2026 standardisation finding.

### Figure 59.12 — Analytical Toolbox
HPLC, LC-MS/proteomics, capillary electrophoresis, enzyme assay, moisture, contaminant testing, and chemometrics as complementary methods.

### Figure 59.13 — Colony Welfare and Recovery
Collection session → temporary defensive activation → rest/recovery → assessment of mortality, foraging, temperament, and colony condition before another session.

### Figure 59.14 — Medical Evidence Boundary
Research on venom components → regulated pharmaceutical development → clinical trials/authorisation; cross out raw-hive-venom injection and deliberate self-stinging as substitutes.

## Accuracy Requirements

- Sting anatomy must be correct.
- Do not hard-code one universal venom composition percentage.
- Do not provide electrical circuit diagrams, exact DIY stimulation settings, or exposed high-voltage construction details.
- Commercial collection must show guarded purpose-built equipment and manufacturer/SOP use.
- Raw venom must never be depicted as injectable medicine.
- Venom immunotherapy must be shown as specialist medical treatment using standardised medicinal preparations.
- Melittin must not be presented as sufficient alone for full quality assessment.
- Anaphylaxis must be treated as a medical emergency.

## Accessibility

- Use labels, shapes, and patterns in addition to colour.
- Reaction-severity graphics must not rely on red/green coding alone.
- Chemical names should be paired with plain-language roles.
- Electrical-safety figures should remain understandable without technical circuit knowledge.
