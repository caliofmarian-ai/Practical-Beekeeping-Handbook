# Chapter 52 — Honey Quality: Illustration Plan

Status: planned for final layout. No provisional artwork is embedded in the reader-facing manuscript.

## Planned Figures

### Figure 52.1 — The Dimensions of Honey Quality
Wheel showing composition, food safety, sensory quality, freshness/processing history, authenticity, botanical origin, geographical origin, traceability and packaging integrity.

### Figure 52.2 — Legal Standard Versus Premium Specification
Two-tier diagram showing legal compositional boundaries and an optional tighter buyer/producer specification inside them. Make clear that stricter commercial limits are not automatically legal limits.

### Figure 52.3 — Core Physicochemical Measurements
Honey sample branching to moisture/refractometry, water activity, sugars/HPLC, free acidity/titration, conductivity, HMF and diastase.

### Figure 52.4 — HMF and Diastase Interpretation
Conceptual paired chart showing heat/storage exposure tending to increase HMF and reduce enzyme activity, with natural botanical variation indicated. No exact heating-history reconstruction.

### Figure 52.5 — Conductivity and Honey Type
Broad classification diagram for many blossom honeys versus honeydew/chestnut categories around the current EU criterion, with a prominent botanical-exceptions label.

### Figure 52.6 — Crystallisation, Phase Separation, and Fermentation
Three clearly distinct states with arrows showing that normal crystallisation is not spoilage, while phase separation can create a wetter liquid layer and increase fermentation risk.

### Figure 52.7 — Sensory Quality Wheel
Aroma, flavour, aftertaste, texture, colour and defect notes, including smoke, fermentation, chemical, metallic, stale and overheated defects.

### Figure 52.8 — Pollen Analysis
Microscope-style panel showing multiple pollen morphologies and a decision note explaining under-/over-representation; pollen percentage is not automatically nectar percentage.

### Figure 52.9 — Authenticity Toolbox
Stable isotopes, LC-IRMS, NMR, GC/LC-MS, sugar profile, pollen, DNA and traceability shown as complementary tools rather than one universal fraud test.

### Figure 52.10 — Country-Origin Traceability
Supplier/apiary lots → weighed blend → country percentages → final lot → 2026 EU label example. Clearly time-date the jurisdictional requirement.

### Figure 52.11 — Certificate of Analysis Anatomy
Annotated CoA showing lot ID, method, units, result, specification, uncertainty/LOQ where relevant, laboratory identity and accreditation scope.

### Figure 52.12 — Representative Sampling
Homogeneous tank, phase-separated tank and multiple drums demonstrating why sampling design controls the value of laboratory results.

### Figure 52.13 — OOS Investigation Flow
Hold lot → verify sample/identity/method/calculation → review process history → justified retest if procedure permits → release/rework/downgrade/reject. Explicitly prohibit repeated testing until a passing number appears.

### Figure 52.14 — Home Authenticity Myths
Crossed-out flame, water-spread, paper, ant, thumb and crystallisation “tests” with caption explaining that genuine honeys vary naturally in physical behaviour.

## Accuracy Requirements

- All numerical legal criteria must be labelled as current jurisdiction-specific examples, not universal values.
- HMF, diastase, conductivity, pollen or colour must never be portrayed as standalone proof of authenticity or exact origin.
- Crystallisation must not be depicted as evidence of added sugar.
- “Not detected” must not be illustrated as absolute zero.
- Laboratory authenticity methods must be shown as complementary and reference-data dependent.
- Infant honey risk should be described accurately as spore-related and not solved by routine honey processing.

## Accessibility

- Use labels, patterns and symbols in addition to colour.
- Analytical charts must work in greyscale.
- Numerical examples should be readable in print and accompanied by explanatory captions.
