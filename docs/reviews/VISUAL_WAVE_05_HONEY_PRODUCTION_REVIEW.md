## Scientific and editorial controls

- Nectar secretion is shown as species/cultivar/environment dependent; flowering is not equated automatically with a colony-level flow.
- Workforce preparation is shown as preceding peak bloom; no universal phenological dates are imposed.
- Supering is based on usable storage and colony/flow context, not empty box volume alone.
- Capping is treated as maturity evidence, not a direct moisture measurement.
- Refractometer figures require representative sampling and instrument control rather than a one-drop/one-number shortcut.
- Harvest readiness includes treatment/restriction, brood and colony-store checks; no universal moisture target is embedded in the artwork.
- Extraction figures separate people/product safety from machine-specific procedures.
- Heating is shown only as a justified, controlled processing step; no arbitrary temperature/time prescription is introduced.
- Food safety, quality and shelf life remain distinct concepts.
- Hygroscopicity is shown as moisture exchange, not one-way absorption under every condition.
- Comb-honey defects are differentiated by significance; presentation defects are not labelled automatically as safety failures.

## Current legal/reference cross-checks

- **Codex Alimentarius CXS 12-1981 — Standard for Honey**: current Codex listing in 2026 records the standard as 2022. The standard includes honey presentation definitions, moisture criteria and analytical references.
- **Council Directive 2001/110/EC relating to honey — consolidated version applicable from 14 June 2026**: used only as an EU example for current honey definitions/composition rules.
- **Directive (EU) 2024/1438**: Member States apply the relevant amendments from 14 June 2026.
