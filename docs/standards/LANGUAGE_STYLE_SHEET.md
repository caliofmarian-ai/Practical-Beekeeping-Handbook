# Language Style Sheet — Practical Beekeeping Handbook

**Edition:** 2026 manuscript  
**Language:** British English  
**Status:** Final copyedit authority for layout

This sheet resolves book-wide spelling, punctuation, terminology, scientific-name, numbering, and editorial conventions. Exact official organisation names, publication titles, quoted text, legal titles, registered product names, and DOI metadata retain their official spelling even when it differs from house style.

## 1. British English

Use British forms in original prose:

- behaviour, not behavior;
- organisation / organise / organised, not organization / organize / organized;
- analyse / analysed, not analyze / analyzed;
- colour, not color;
- centre, not center;
- litre, not liter;
- metre, not meter, except measuring instruments such as a meter where technically conventional;
- labelled / labelling / unlabelled, not labeled / labeling / unlabeled;
- programme, not program, except computer program/software program where the computing sense requires it;
- licence (noun), license (verb), licensed (verb/adjective);
- travelling / travelled;
- fulfilment, not fulfillment;
- mould, not mold;
- odour, not odor;
- grey / greyscale, not gray / grayscale;
- ageing, not aging, unless preserving an exact technical title/source spelling.

### Proper-name exception

Do not “correct” official names or titles such as:

- Food and Agriculture **Organization** of the United Nations;
- World Health **Organization**;
- Centers for Disease Control and Prevention;
- journal/book titles using American spelling;
- quoted regulatory text.

## 2. Chapter Titles

Canonical title format in final layout:

`Chapter 5 — Modern Beekeeping`

Use an em dash (`—`) between chapter number and title in the final formatted edition.

Legacy manuscript files with an en dash (`–`) remain semantically valid; Issue #106 must normalise displayed chapter headings during layout/export.

## 3. Heading Style

Use sentence-style conceptual wording but title case for Markdown headings where already established by the manuscript.

Examples:

- `## Learning Objectives`
- `## Key Takeaways`
- `## Chapter Summary`
- `## Review Questions`
- `## References`

Numbered chapter subsections retain the established form:

`## 41.18 Calculating Mites per 100 Bees`

Do not add terminal full stops to headings.

## 4. Colony and Hive

Preferred distinction:

- **colony** — the living social unit: queen, workers, drones when present, brood, stores, and colony organisation;
- **hive** — the physical housing/equipment.

Ordinary phrases such as “open the hive” are acceptable when physical equipment is meant.

Avoid phrases that make the wooden box biologically act, e.g. prefer “the colony is queenless” to “the hive is queenless”.

## 5. Bee Health Terminology

Use terms biologically:

- **infection** — pathogen present/replicating;
- **disease** — harmful clinical/pathological expression;
- **parasite** — lives on/in a host at host expense;
- **pathogen** — infectious disease agent;
- **pest/scavenger** — damages nest/comb/stores but is not necessarily a parasite;
- **predator** — captures/consumes bees;
- **infestation** — parasite/pest presence and burden.

Do not use `disease` as a catch-all for Varroa, pesticides, starvation, or predators.

## 6. Scientific Names

Italicise genus/species:

- *Apis mellifera*;
- *Varroa destructor*;
- *Paenibacillus larvae*;
- *Melissococcus plutonius*;
- *Ascosphaera apis*;
- *Acarapis woodi*;
- *Tropilaelaps mercedesae*.

After first full use within a local context, abbreviated genus is acceptable where unambiguous: *V. destructor*, *P. larvae*.

Common practical names remain roman type: Varroa, Tropilaelaps, Nosema, small hive beetle.

## 7. Nosema / Vairimorpha

House approach:

- use **Nosema** in practical beekeeper prose because it remains widely recognised;
- acknowledge the **Vairimorpha** reclassification where taxonomic precision matters;
- do not alternate randomly between names sentence by sentence.

## 8. Abbreviations

Expand at first meaningful use in a chapter unless universally obvious in immediate context.

Examples:

- American foulbrood (AFB);
- European foulbrood (EFB);
- deformed wing virus (DWV);
- chronic bee paralysis virus (CBPV);
- integrated pest management (IPM);
- food business operator (FBO);
- Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Point (HACCP);
- hydroxymethylfurfural (HMF).

The Reference 75 glossary may list abbreviations without re-expanding each chapter.

## 9. Numbers and Units

- Use metric units as primary.
- Insert a space between number and unit: `20 °C`, `5 kg`, `300 mL`, `3 km`.
- Use `%` without a space in ordinary running text only where the manuscript already follows that convention consistently; layout may normalise to publisher style.
- Use decimal points, not decimal commas in the English edition.
- Avoid unnecessary precision.
- Spell out ordinary whole numbers where prose reads better, but keep numerals for measurements, tables, formulas, dates, numbered steps, and technical comparisons.

## 10. Dates

Preferred prose format:

`16 September 2026`

ISO form is acceptable in identifiers and data records:

`2026-09-16`

Do not use ambiguous all-numeric `09/10/2026` date formats in reader-facing text.

## 11. Legal and Regulatory Statements

When a rule can change, include the jurisdiction and date/context.

Preferred patterns:

- “Under the EU rules applicable from 14 June 2026…”
- “Current Irish guidance requires…”
- “Authorised products vary by jurisdiction.”

Avoid timeless wording such as “the law says” without specifying which law/market.

## 12. Safety Language

Use direct imperative language for critical actions:

- **Stop movement.**
- **Do not blend the suspect lot.**
- **Seek emergency medical assistance.**
- **Isolate power before opening the extractor.**

Avoid euphemistic safety wording where ambiguity could cause harm.

## 13. Treatment and Product Language

Use:

- authorised product;
- label directions;
- active ingredient;
- withdrawal/harvest restriction;
- current regional guidance;
- validated method.

Avoid:

- “safe natural treatment” without qualification;
- “chemical-free” as a scientific description;
- generic homemade treatment recipes;
- wording implying an organic-permitted substance is automatically a legally authorised veterinary product.

## 14. Diagnosis Language

Preferred hierarchy:

- “may indicate” / “raises suspicion” for non-specific field signs;
- “supports the diagnosis” for validated supporting evidence;
- “confirmed” only when the text has defined what confirmation means in that context.

Avoid “proves” unless the evidence genuinely warrants it.

## 15. Honey Quality Language

Keep these distinct:

- **moisture content** — percentage water measured commonly by refractometry;
- **water activity** — available water for microbial/chemical processes;
- **legal maximum/minimum** — jurisdictional compliance value;
- **operational target** — producer/buyer quality specification;
- **authenticity** — truthful identity/composition/origin;
- **quality** — sensory/physicochemical/commercial properties;
- **food safety** — unacceptable hazards.

Do not use one concept as a synonym for another.

## 16. Organic Language

Use **organic** only when referring to a certified/legally defined system or clearly describing the regulation.

Do not use `organic` as a synonym for:

- natural;
- untreated;
- local;
- low-input;
- pesticide-free.

### Technical Review T01 copyedit directive

Chapter 70 §70.23 final displayed text should explicitly include the full example list:

- organic honey;
- **organic pollen**;
- organic sugar syrups;
- organic sugar.

This is a completeness correction from Technical Review #104.

## 17. Health Claims

Separate:

- chemical composition;
- in-vitro activity;
- animal research;
- human clinical evidence;
- authorised health claim;
- authorised medicinal indication.

Never turn phrases such as “antimicrobial in laboratory assays” into “treats infection”.

## 18. Hyphenation

Preferred established forms:

- honey bee (two words);
- beekeeper, beekeeping;
- brood nest;
- queen-right / queenless where adjectival use requires clarity;
- food-grade;
- food-contact;
- colony-level;
- apiary-level;
- post-treatment;
- pre-treatment;
- time–temperature (en dash for paired technical relationship);
- Varroa-associated;
- wax-moth where compound adjective needs clarity; `wax moth` as noun.

Avoid excessive hyphenation when the phrase is unambiguous.

## 19. Dashes

- Em dash `—`: strong sentence interruption and chapter-title separator.
- En dash `–`: numerical ranges and paired relationships where typographically appropriate, e.g. `2025–2026`, `Varroa–DWV interaction`, `time–temperature`.
- Hyphen `-`: compound words and identifiers.

Do not use a hyphen in place of a true range dash in final typesetting.

## 20. Quotations

Use curly quotation marks in final layout where the typesetting system supports them.

Retain exact spelling within quoted official text.

Do not use scare quotes excessively; define the term instead.

## 21. Lists

Parallel grammar is preferred.

If a list begins with verbs, keep all items as verbs.

Example:

- inspect brood;
- measure Varroa;
- record food status;
- schedule follow-up.

Avoid mixing sentences, noun fragments, and commands in the same short list unless the content requires it.

## 22. Review Questions

Questions remain concise, direct, and answerable from the chapter.

Avoid:

- trick questions;
- introducing new facts not taught in the chapter;
- multiple unrelated questions hidden under one number;
- answers embedded in the wording.

The existing target of 100 review questions per major chapter remains intact.

## 23. References

Preserve the original spelling of:

- article titles;
- book titles;
- journal names;
- organisation names.

Normalise surrounding citation punctuation and order during final bibliography/layout, but never “Britishise” a publication title.

## 24. Words Flagged During Language Pass 1

Known original-prose American forms to normalise in final source/layout:

- Ch. 34: `organized` → `organised` where not quoted;
- Ch. 35: `organize`, `organization`, `analyze`, `analyzed` → British forms;
- Ch. 36: `analyze`, `organized` → British forms;
- Ch. 37: `organized`, `organization` → British forms;
- Ch. 66: `unlabeled` → `unlabelled`;
- illustration/reference production text: `grayscale` → `greyscale` under house style.

Proper nouns and original publication titles containing American spellings remain unchanged.

## 25. Final Layout Rule

Issue #106 must apply the style sheet consistently to reader-facing output. The layout/build process should not silently rewrite:

- URLs;
- DOI strings;
- code/file paths;
- legal titles;
- product names;
- scientific names;
- quoted text.

All automated replacements require a reviewable exception list.
