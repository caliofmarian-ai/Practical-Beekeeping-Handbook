# Writing Standards

**Project:** Practical Beekeeping Handbook

**Version:** 1.0

---

# Mission

The goal of this handbook is to provide one of the most practical, accurate, and accessible beekeeping references available in English.

The handbook shall combine scientific knowledge, practical field experience, modern beekeeping techniques, and original educational illustrations to create a resource suitable for both beginners and experienced beekeepers.

---

# Target Audience

This handbook is intended for:

- Beginners
- Hobby beekeepers
- Commercial beekeepers
- Students
- Teachers
- Researchers
- Anyone interested in honey bees and modern apiculture

---

# Writing Style

All handbook content shall follow these rules:

- Use clear international English.
- Prefer short, direct sentences.
- Explain technical terms when they first appear.
- Avoid unnecessary jargon.
- Avoid exaggerated or promotional claims.
- Separate verified facts from practical recommendations.
- State uncertainty when evidence is limited.
- Use consistent terminology throughout the handbook.
- Write for practical use in the apiary, not only for theory.

---

# Required Chapter Structure

Each chapter should contain, where relevant:

1. Chapter title
2. Learning objectives
3. Main explanation
4. Practical application
5. Practical tips
6. Common mistakes
7. Safety warnings
8. Scientific notes
9. Tables, diagrams, or illustrations
10. Summary
11. Key takeaways
12. Practical checklist
13. References

---

# Practical Focus

Every chapter must answer the question:

> How can the reader apply this information in a real apiary?

The handbook should include concrete procedures, decision points, warning signs, examples, and field-ready checklists whenever possible.

---

# Safety Rules

Whenever a subject involves risk, the chapter must include a clearly marked safety section.

Relevant risks may include:

- Bee stings
- Severe allergic reactions
- Smoke and fire
- Sharp tools
- Heavy hive components
- Chemical treatments
- Food contamination
- Infectious bee diseases
- Transport and public safety

Safety information must be practical, specific, and proportionate to the actual risk.

---

# Accuracy and Evidence

Scientific, medical, legal, regulatory, and food-safety claims must be supported by reliable sources.

Preferred sources include:

- Peer-reviewed scientific papers
- Universities
- Government agencies
- Official legislation
- Recognised bee-health institutes
- Professional beekeeping organisations

Unverified claims, folklore, and unsupported remedies must not be presented as established fact.

---

# Originality

All text, diagrams, tables, and educational explanations must be original or properly licensed.

The handbook must not copy substantial passages from other books, articles, websites, or training materials.

Sources should be paraphrased accurately and cited properly.

---

# Visual Elements

Figures, diagrams, photographs, and tables must:

- Support the text directly
- Have a clear purpose
- Use consistent numbering
- Include a descriptive caption
- Be referenced in the chapter text
- Use original or properly licensed material

Numbering examples:

- Figure 4.2
- Diagram 7.1
- Table 9.3
- Checklist 5.1

---

# Quality Standard

Each chapter should be strong enough to function as a useful standalone practical guide while remaining consistent with the rest of the handbook.

Before a chapter is marked complete, it must be checked for:

- Accuracy
- Clarity
- Practical value
- Consistent terminology
- Safety
- References
- Originality
- Visual support
- Grammar
- Formatting
