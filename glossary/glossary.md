# Reference 75 — Glossary

This glossary defines the principal technical, biological, practical, food-production, business, regulatory, and research terms used throughout the *Practical Beekeeping Handbook*. Definitions are written for operational clarity rather than as exhaustive dictionary entries. Scientific names are italicised. Where terminology varies by region, the most broadly accepted term is used and alternatives are noted.

## A

**Abdomen** — The posterior major body region of an adult honey bee, containing much of the digestive, reproductive, respiratory, wax-producing, and stinging anatomy.

**Absconding** — Departure of nearly the entire colony from the nest, usually in response to severe disturbance, chronic stress, pests, overheating, starvation, or unsuitable nest conditions. It differs from reproductive swarming.

**Action threshold** — A monitoring result or risk condition at which management action is justified. Thresholds can vary by pest, season, climate, colony condition, and jurisdiction.

**Active ingredient** — The chemical or biological component of a treatment responsible for its principal effect. Different brands may contain the same active ingredient.

**Adult bee** — A fully emerged honey bee: queen, worker, or drone.

**Africanised honey bee** — Populations of *Apis mellifera* with ancestry derived partly from African honey bee lineages introduced into the Americas. Behaviour and legal status vary by region.

**AFB** — American foulbrood; a serious bacterial brood disease caused by *Paenibacillus larvae*.

**Apiary** — A site where one or more managed honey bee colonies are kept.

**Apiary carrying capacity** — The number and strength of colonies that a landscape can support without excessive forage competition, considering floral resources, season, weather, and other pollinators.

**Apiary inspection record** — A dated record of colony condition, brood, queen status, food, health observations, interventions, and follow-up actions.

**Apiary registration** — Registration of beekeepers, apiaries, or colonies required or offered by a competent authority in some jurisdictions.

**Apiculture** — The science and practice of managing honey bees.

**Apis mellifera** — The western honey bee, the principal managed honey bee species in Europe, Africa, the Americas, Australia, and many other regions.

**Apis cerana** — The eastern or Asian honey bee, naturally distributed across much of Asia and the original host associated with *Varroa* mites before host switching to *A. mellifera*.

**Apitoxin** — Bee venom; the secretion delivered through the worker honey bee sting apparatus.

**ATC** — Automatic temperature compensation; a refractometer function that compensates for instrument-temperature effects within a specified operating range. It does not correct poor calibration or bad sampling.

**Authenticity** — Whether a product is genuinely what it claims to be in composition, botanical origin, geographical origin, production method, and absence of undeclared adulterants.

## B

**Backfilling** — Storage of nectar or honey in cells within the brood nest that might otherwise be available for egg laying.

**Bait hive** — An unoccupied hive or box intentionally placed to attract a natural swarm.

**Bee bread** — Pollen stored in comb and transformed through packing, nectar/honey addition, microbial activity, and maturation inside the colony.

**Bee brush** — A soft brush used to remove bees gently from frames or other surfaces.

**Bee escape** — A one-way device that allows bees to leave a super while limiting return, commonly used before honey harvest.

**Bee space** — The characteristic gap that honey bees tend to leave open as a passage rather than fill with comb or propolis, commonly around 6–9 mm depending on context and equipment.

**Bee venom** — A complex secretion from the sting apparatus containing peptides, enzymes, and other compounds, including melittin and phospholipase A2.

**Beeswax** — Wax secreted by worker honey bee abdominal glands and used to construct comb.

**Best-before date** — A date-marking concept primarily related to food quality rather than a universal acute safety cut-off; exact legal meaning is jurisdiction-specific.

**Biosecurity** — Measures that reduce introduction and spread of pests, pathogens, parasites, and contaminated material among colonies, apiaries, and operations.

**Biotechnical control** — Pest-management technique that manipulates colony biology rather than relying solely on a pesticide; examples include brood interruption and drone-brood removal.

**Blend** — A deliberate mixture of two or more honey lots or other compatible product lots, with traceability retained for every source.

**Bloom** — Flowering stage of a plant population; in beekeeping, often used in nectar-flow and phenology records.

**Brood** — Honey bee eggs, larvae, and pupae before adult emergence.

**Brood break** — A period when a colony has no brood or no capped brood, naturally or through management. It can affect parasite reproduction and treatment opportunities.

**Brood chamber** — Hive area intended primarily for brood rearing and colony stores associated with the brood nest.

**Brood comb** — Comb used for brood rearing; repeated brood cycles leave cocoons and residues in cells.

**Brood food** — Glandular and nutritional secretions fed to developing larvae by nurse bees.

**Brood nest** — The area of comb containing brood, usually surrounded by pollen and food reserves.

**Brood pattern** — Spatial arrangement and age distribution of brood on comb; useful as one indicator of queen performance, brood viability, disease, parasites, and colony stress.

**Burr comb** — Comb built in spaces outside normal frame alignment, often where bee space is incorrect.

## C

**Capped brood** — Developing brood sealed beneath wax cappings during pupal development.

**Capped honey** — Honey sealed under wax cappings by bees after ripening. Capping is a useful maturity indicator but not a precise moisture measurement.

**Capping scratcher** — Tool used to open recessed capped honey cells before extraction.

**Cappings** — Thin wax covers removed from sealed honey cells before extraction; often a high-quality beeswax source.

**Cell** — A hexagonal wax compartment in comb used for brood rearing or storage of honey and pollen.

**Certificate of analysis (CoA)** — Laboratory or supplier document reporting defined test results for an identified product lot.

**Chain of custody** — Documented control of a sample or product as it passes between people, locations, and processes.

**Chalkbrood** — Fungal brood disease caused by *Ascosphaera apis*, producing characteristic mummified larvae.

**Chilled brood** — Brood injured or killed by inadequate adult coverage or exposure to low temperature.

**Chronic bee paralysis virus (CBPV)** — Honey bee virus associated with trembling, crawling, flightlessness, adult mortality, and sometimes dark hairless-looking bees.

**Clean-to-dirty sequence** — Inspection order in which apparently healthy colonies are handled before suspect or diseased colonies to reduce cross-contamination.

**Cleansing flight** — Flight during which bees defecate outside the hive, particularly important after prolonged confinement.

**Clinical disease** — Disease with observable signs or measurable pathology, as distinct from subclinical infection.

**Cluster** — Dense grouping of bees, especially during cold conditions when workers regulate heat collectively.

**Cold chain** — Temperature-controlled handling system used to preserve heat-sensitive hive products such as royal jelly and some pollen products.

**Colony** — The living social unit of honey bees, including queen, workers, drones when present, brood, stores, and nest environment.

**Colony collapse** — Severe failure of colony population or function. It is a descriptive outcome and should not automatically be attributed to one cause.

**Colony density** — Number of colonies within a defined area; relevant to forage competition, disease transmission, and apiary planning.

**Colony identification** — Unique code or number that links inspections, treatments, queen history, harvests, movements, and traceability records to a specific colony.

**Comb** — Structure of beeswax cells constructed by honey bees.

**Comb honey** — Honey sold in freshly built broodless comb or sections of such comb, according to applicable product definitions.

**Commensal organism** — Organism associated with a host or colony without necessarily causing significant harm.

**Composite sample** — Sample created by combining defined portions from multiple locations or containers to estimate average lot properties.

**Conductivity** — Electrical conductivity of a honey solution; used as a compositional indicator related to mineral and organic-acid content and useful in broad honey classification.

**Contact dermatitis** — Skin inflammation caused by irritant or allergic contact; propolis is a recognised allergen for some people.

**Controlled crystallisation** — Deliberate management of nucleation and crystal growth to produce a defined honey texture, such as creamed honey.

**Conversion period** — Required period during which an operation transitions to certified organic status under a defined organic standard.

**Creamed honey** — Honey with deliberately controlled fine crystallisation, producing a smooth spreadable texture; not honey containing dairy cream.

**Cross-comb** — Comb built across frames or guides rather than within intended frame alignment.

**Cross-contamination** — Transfer of biological, chemical, physical, or allergen contamination from one colony, product, tool, surface, or process to another.

**Cut comb** — Comb honey produced in larger combs and cut into retail portions after harvest.

## D

**Deformed wing virus (DWV)** — Major honey bee RNA virus strongly associated with *Varroa destructor* transmission and disease expression.

**Dehumidification** — Controlled removal of water from air, comb, or product. Legal acceptability of direct honey-moisture reduction methods can vary by jurisdiction.

**Dearth** — Period when nectar availability is insufficient relative to colony needs.

**Dead-out** — Colony found dead or abandoned; should be protected from robbing until the cause is assessed.

**Diastase** — Honey amylase enzyme activity used as one quality indicator affected by botanical origin, heat, and storage.

**Differential diagnosis** — Systematic comparison of multiple possible causes of the same signs before assigning a diagnosis.

**Disease** — Harmful condition caused by an infectious agent or pathological process.

**Drone** — Male honey bee, produced from an unfertilised egg, whose principal biological role is mating with queens.

**Drone brood** — Developing male brood, normally reared in larger cells and capped longer than worker brood.

**Drone-brood removal** — Biotechnical Varroa-control method that removes capped drone brood containing reproducing mites before drone emergence.

**Drifting** — Entry of a bee into a colony other than its own, contributing to movement of parasites and pathogens.

**Dry capping** — Honey capping with an air space between wax and honey, often appearing white; not a stand-alone measure of purity or moisture.

**Dysentery** — Visible faecal staining or abnormal defecation; a sign with multiple possible causes and not a synonym for Nosema disease.

## E

**EFB** — European foulbrood; bacterial brood disease caused by *Melissococcus plutonius*.

**Economic injury level** — Pest density at which economic loss equals the cost of control in classical IPM; beekeeping decisions can also involve legal, biological, welfare, and pollination considerations.

**Ectoparasite** — Parasite living externally on a host, such as *Varroa destructor*.

**Egg** — Earliest honey bee developmental stage; queen-laid worker and queen eggs are fertilised, while drone eggs normally develop from unfertilised eggs.

**Electrical conductivity** — See **conductivity**.

**Endospore** — Highly resistant bacterial survival structure. *Paenibacillus larvae*, the AFB organism, forms exceptionally persistent endospores.

**Entomopathogenic** — Capable of causing disease in insects; used for fungi, bacteria, viruses, or other agents studied as biological controls.

**Ethanol extract** — Extract made using ethanol as solvent; common in propolis processing and requiring flammable-liquid controls.

**European foulbrood** — See **EFB**.

**Excluder** — See **queen excluder**.

**Extraction** — Removal of honey from uncapped broodless comb, usually by centrifugal force; or, in hive-product processing, transfer of selected compounds into a solvent.

**Extractor** — Machine that removes honey from comb centrifugally.

**Extrafloral nectary** — Nectar-secreting plant structure located outside flowers.

## F

**Fat body** — Honey bee tissue involved in nutrient storage, metabolism, immunity, detoxification, and longevity; an important feeding target of *Varroa destructor*.

**Feed conversion** — Relationship between supplemental feed supplied and colony biological or production response; not a fixed universal ratio.

**Feeder** — Device used to provide liquid or solid supplemental feed to a colony.

**Fermentation** — Microbial conversion of honey sugars, usually by osmotolerant yeasts when moisture and temperature permit, producing gas and off-flavours.

**Filtered honey** — In some legal systems, a defined honey category in which filtration significantly removes pollen; ordinary coarse straining should not automatically be equated with this category.

**Flow** — See **nectar flow**.

**Food business operator (FBO)** — Person or business legally responsible for ensuring compliance with food law within the food business under applicable jurisdictional definitions.

**Food-contact material** — Material intended to contact food, such as stainless steel, food-grade plastics, hoses, seals, or packaging.

**Forager** — Worker bee engaged in collecting nectar, pollen, water, or resin outside the nest.

**Forage** — Floral or other landscape resources used by bees, principally nectar, pollen, resin, and water.

**Foraging radius** — Distance over which bees search for resources. It varies with landscape and profitability and should not be treated as a uniformly used circle.

**Foundation** — Manufactured sheet or base, often beeswax or plastic, used to guide comb construction.

**Foundationless frame** — Frame in which bees build comb without a full manufactured foundation sheet, usually from a guide or starter strip.

**Free acidity** — Titrated acidity of honey, distinct from pH, used as a compositional quality measure.

**Fresh nectar** — Recently collected floral nectar that has not yet undergone full ripening into honey.

**Fumagillin** — Antimicrobial compound historically used against Nosema in some jurisdictions. Legal status and permitted uses vary and must be verified locally.

## G

**Genetic diversity** — Variation in genetic composition within or among populations; important for adaptation, disease resistance, breeding, and conservation.

**Genetic stock** — Honey bee breeding population selected or maintained for defined traits.

**Grooming** — Behaviour in which bees remove particles or parasites from themselves or nestmates.

**Growing degree days** — Accumulated heat units used to model development of plants or pests; useful only when correctly calibrated.

## H

**HACCP** — Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Point; a structured food-safety approach based on hazard identification, preventive controls, monitoring, corrective action, verification, and records.

**Haemocytometer** — Calibrated counting chamber used for microscopic counts, such as Nosema spore estimates.

**Haemolymph** — Insect circulatory fluid.

**Hazard** — Biological, chemical, physical, or allergen agent or condition with potential to cause harm.

**Hazard analysis** — Process of identifying hazards, assessing their significance, and determining appropriate controls.

**Headspace** — Air volume above a product inside a closed tank or package.

**Heather honey** — Honey from *Calluna* or related heather sources; some legal standards provide specific compositional exceptions.

**Hive** — Artificial nest structure used to house a managed honey bee colony.

**Hive body** — Box containing frames in a modular hive system.

**Hive beetle** — Usually small hive beetle, *Aethina tumida*, a hive pest whose legal status varies by region.

**Hive scale** — Scale used to measure whole-hive mass over time for nectar-flow, swarm, feeding, and consumption monitoring.

**Hive tool** — Metal or other durable tool used to separate hive parts, scrape propolis, and manipulate frames.

**HMF** — Hydroxymethylfurfural, a honey compound that tends to increase with heating and storage and is used as a quality-history indicator.

**Honey** — Natural sweet substance produced by honey bees from plant nectar, honeydew, or related plant secretions, transformed and stored in comb according to applicable product definitions.

**Honeydew** — Sugar-rich excretion of sap-feeding insects collected by bees and converted into honeydew honey.

**Honey house** — Food-handling area used for extraction, processing, storage, or packing of honey.

**Honey super** — Hive box intended mainly for storage of harvestable honey above or outside the primary brood chamber.

**Horizontal transmission** — Transmission of a pathogen between individuals of the same generation or social group.

**Hygienic behaviour** — Worker behaviour involving detection, uncapping, and removal of abnormal brood.

**Hygroscopic** — Able to absorb or exchange moisture with surrounding air; honey is strongly hygroscopic.

## I

**IBC** — Intermediate bulk container; large food-grade container used for storage or transport when suitable for honey.

**Immigration of mites** — Entry of Varroa mites into a colony from outside sources through drifting, robbing, or movement of bees and brood.

**Imidacloprid** — Neonicotinoid insecticide often discussed in pollinator-risk research; legal uses vary by jurisdiction.

**Immune response** — Physiological defence mechanisms of an individual bee against infection or injury.

**Infestation** — Presence and population of parasites or pests on or within a host or colony.

**Infection** — Presence and replication of an infectious agent in a host; infection does not always produce clinical disease.

**Integrated pest management (IPM)** — Evidence-based system combining prevention, monitoring, thresholds or regulatory triggers, cultural/genetic/physical/biological controls, authorised treatments, resistance management, and verification.

**Invertase** — Honey bee enzyme involved in conversion of sucrose into glucose and fructose; heat-sensitive and used in some honey-quality research.

**Isotope-ratio analysis** — Laboratory technique using natural isotope patterns to investigate authenticity or origin, including some forms of sugar adulteration.

## J

**Jenter system** — Queen-rearing system that allows eggs or newly hatched larvae to be transferred from a queen-confined laying grid without conventional manual grafting.

## K

**K-wing** — Abnormal wing posture historically associated with tracheal-mite infestation but not diagnostic on its own.

## L

**Larva** — Feeding developmental stage between egg and pupa.

**Lateral-flow test** — Portable immunological field test used for some bee diseases; interpretation depends on validated sampling and regulatory context.

**Laying worker** — Worker with activated ovaries that lays unfertilised eggs, usually after prolonged queenlessness.

**Lot** — Defined quantity of product manufactured, harvested, processed, or packed under conditions allowing it to be identified and traced.

**Lot code** — Unique identifier linking a product unit or batch to source and processing records.

**Lyophilisation** — Freeze drying; water removal from a frozen product under vacuum, used for some hive products such as royal jelly.

## M

**Mass balance** — Accounting of material entering, leaving, being blended, lost, or remaining in a process; important for traceability and fraud prevention.

**Melissopalynology** — Study of pollen contained in honey, used to support botanical and geographical origin assessment.

**Melittin** — Major peptide component of honey bee venom. Its concentration alone does not define the complete quality or biological profile of venom.

**Metamorphosis** — Developmental transformation through egg, larva, pupa, and adult stages.

**Microbiome** — Community of microorganisms associated with a host or environment.

**Microsporidia** — Obligate intracellular fungus-related organisms including the agents historically called *Nosema apis* and *Nosema ceranae*.

**Mite wash** — Quantitative Varroa-monitoring method using alcohol, detergent, or another validated wash to dislodge mites from a measured adult-bee sample.

**Moisture content** — Percentage of water in honey or another product, important for viscosity, fermentation risk, stability, and legal composition.

**Monofloral honey** — Honey derived wholly or mainly from one floral source and having the expected sensory, physicochemical, and microscopic characteristics under applicable standards.

**MRJP** — Major royal jelly protein; group of proteins abundant in royal jelly and relevant to composition and quality research.

## N

**Nectar** — Sugar-rich plant secretion collected by bees and converted into honey.

**Nectar flow** — Period when colony nectar intake exceeds immediate colony consumption strongly enough for surplus storage.

**Nectary** — Plant structure that secretes nectar.

**Nosema** — Traditional genus name widely used for honey bee microsporidia *N. apis* and *N. ceranae*; some current taxonomy uses *Vairimorpha*.

**Nosemosis** — Disease or infection associated principally with *Nosema/Vairimorpha apis* and *N. / V. ceranae*.

**Nuc** — Short for nucleus colony; small functional colony containing bees, brood, food, and normally a queen or queen-rearing potential.

**Nurse bee** — Worker performing brood-care functions, typically during early adult life.

## O

**Organic beekeeping** — Certified production system governed by defined organic rules; not a synonym for treatment-free or pesticide-free beekeeping.

**Orientation flight** — Flight pattern by which young or relocated bees learn hive location and landscape cues.

**Osmotolerant yeast** — Yeast capable of growing in high-sugar environments and responsible for honey fermentation under suitable moisture conditions.

**Out-of-specification (OOS)** — Result that falls outside an established legal, customer, or internal specification and requires controlled investigation.

## P

**Package bees** — Shipped adult bees supplied with or without a queen, usually without comb or brood.

**Parthenogenesis** — Development from an unfertilised egg; drones arise by arrhenotokous parthenogenesis in normal honey bee reproduction.

**Pathogen** — Biological agent capable of causing disease.

**Pesticide** — Substance intended to prevent, destroy, repel, or control a pest; includes insecticides, fungicides, herbicides, acaricides, and others.

**Phenology** — Timing of recurring biological events, such as flowering, brood expansion, or pest development.

**Pheromone** — Chemical signal produced by an organism that affects other members of the same species.

**Phospholipase A2 (PLA2)** — Major enzymatic allergen component of honey bee venom.

**Pollen** — Male reproductive material of seed plants; collected by bees as the principal natural protein, lipid, vitamin, and mineral source for brood rearing.

**Pollen basket** — Corbicula; smooth depression on the hind tibia of a worker bee used to transport compacted pollen or resin.

**Pollen trap** — Device that removes a proportion of pollen pellets from returning foragers for collection.

**Pollination** — Transfer of pollen to reproductive structures of flowers, enabling fertilisation in many plants.

**Pollinator conservation** — Protection and restoration of habitat, forage, nesting resources, and safe landscapes for wild and managed pollinators.

**Propolis** — Bee-processed resinous hive material used to seal, reinforce, and modify nest surfaces and microbial exposure.

**Pupa** — Non-feeding developmental stage between larva and adult.

## Q

**Queen** — Reproductive female honey bee, normally the principal egg-layer in a colony.

**Queen cell** — Special vertically oriented cell in which a queen develops.

**Queen excluder** — Grid allowing worker passage while restricting queen movement based on body size.

**Queenlessness** — State in which a colony lacks a functional queen; should be diagnosed from evidence rather than failure to see the queen once.

**Queen-right** — Colony containing a functional queen.

**Queen rearing** — Controlled production of queen cells and queens through biological and management techniques.

**Queen substance** — Older general term for queen-associated pheromonal signals, especially queen mandibular pheromone.

## R

**Refractometer** — Optical or digital instrument used to estimate honey moisture from refractive index.

**Reinvasion** — Increase in colony parasite pressure caused by mites entering from other colonies after control; often used for Varroa immigration.

**Rendering** — Controlled melting and physical separation of beeswax from comb debris, honey, water, and other materials.

**Representative sample** — Sample collected so its measured properties reasonably reflect the defined lot or population.

**Residue** — Trace amount of a chemical remaining in honey, wax, pollen, or another hive product after treatment or environmental exposure.

**Resistance** — Heritable ability of a pest population to survive a control that previously killed susceptible individuals; or host traits reducing parasite/pathogen success.

**Robbing** — Theft of honey or feed by bees from another colony, often associated with fighting and disease/parasite transfer.

**Royal jelly** — Glandular secretion produced by nurse bees and fed intensively to queen larvae and adult queens; a highly perishable hive product when harvested commercially.

**RT-PCR** — Reverse-transcription polymerase chain reaction, used to detect RNA targets such as honey bee viruses.

## S

**Sacbrood** — Viral brood disease caused by sacbrood virus, producing characteristic larval changes and a sac-like body in affected larvae.

**Scale hive** — See **hive scale**.

**Scout bee** — Worker that searches independently for new nest sites or food sources and communicates discoveries.

**Screened floor** — Hive floor incorporating mesh, used for ventilation, debris monitoring, and some mite-drop observations.

**Seed honey** — Fine-crystallised honey added to liquid honey to control crystal nucleation in creamed-honey production.

**Sensory analysis** — Structured evaluation of aroma, flavour, texture, appearance, and defects using controlled methods.

**Shook swarm** — Management method that moves adult bees from old comb onto clean comb or foundation; used in some disease-control programmes under defined protocols.

**Slumgum** — Solid debris remaining after beeswax rendering, containing cocoons, propolis, pollen, dirt, and residual wax.

**Small hive beetle** — *Aethina tumida*, scavenging hive pest whose larvae can damage comb and ferment honey.

**Social immunity** — Colony-level collective behaviours and nest features that reduce disease or parasite pressure.

**Spore count** — Microscopic estimate of spores in a sample; useful for Nosema investigation but not a universal disease or treatment threshold.

**Starter strip** — Narrow strip of wax or other guide used to encourage straight natural comb construction.

**Stonebrood** — Uncommon fungal brood disease associated with *Aspergillus* species.

**Super** — Hive box placed to provide additional comb space, often for honey storage.

**Supersaturation** — State in which a solution contains more dissolved material than is stable at equilibrium, as with glucose in many honeys before crystallisation.

**Swarm** — Reproductive division of a colony in which a queen and a large group of workers leave to establish a new nest.

**Swarm cell** — Queen cell produced as part of colony reproductive swarming.

**Swarm trap** — See **bait hive**.

**Systematic review** — Research synthesis using predefined methods to identify, assess, and summarise all eligible evidence addressing a defined question.

## T

**Tangential extractor** — Honey extractor holding the broad comb face toward the wall so frames are normally turned or reversed to extract both sides.

**Thermoregulation** — Collective regulation of colony temperature through clustering, heat production, ventilation, water evaporation, and bee distribution.

**Thixotropic honey** — Honey whose structure becomes less viscous when mechanically disturbed and reforms at rest; some heather honeys show this behaviour.

**Traceability** — Ability to identify and follow bees, products, ingredients, treatments, suppliers, customers, or lots through defined stages of production and distribution.

**Tracheal mite** — *Acarapis woodi*, microscopic mite living mainly in adult-bee thoracic tracheae.

**Treatment-free beekeeping** — Management philosophy or practice in which acaricides or other treatments are not used. It is not equivalent to organic certification and should not replace monitoring or animal-welfare obligations.

**Tropilaelaps** — Genus of parasitic brood mites including *T. mercedesae* and *T. clareae*; important emerging threats to *Apis mellifera*.

## U

**Uncapping** — Removal or opening of wax cappings from honey cells before extraction.

**Uncapping knife** — Knife, sometimes heated, used to remove honey cappings.

**Unit cost** — Total relevant cost assigned to one unit of output, such as cost per kilogram of honey or per retail jar.

**Unsealed brood** — Eggs and larvae in cells not yet capped.

## V

**Varietal honey** — Honey marketed for a dominant floral source when evidence and applicable standards support that origin.

**Varroa destructor** — Major ectoparasitic mite of *Apis mellifera*, reproducing in capped brood and transmitting/amplifying important viruses.

**Varroa-sensitive hygiene (VSH)** — Heritable worker behaviour associated with detection and removal of brood containing reproducing Varroa mites.

**Vector** — Organism that transmits a pathogen between hosts; Varroa is an important vector of honey bee viruses.

**Vertical transmission** — Transmission from parent to offspring, including through reproductive tissues or eggs.

**Vitellogenin** — Egg-yolk precursor protein with broader functions in worker honey bee physiology, longevity, nutrition, and social organisation.

## W

**Waggle dance** — Recruitment dance communicating direction and distance of valuable resources or nest sites.

**Water activity (aw)** — Measure of water available for microbial and chemical processes; related to but distinct from total moisture content.

**Wax moth** — Moths, especially *Galleria mellonella* and *Achroia grisella*, whose larvae feed on comb residues and can destroy stored comb.

**Wax scale** — Small plate of newly secreted beeswax produced by worker abdominal wax glands.

**Wet capping** — Honey capping in direct contact with honey, giving a more translucent appearance; not a sign of inferior safety or authenticity by itself.

**Winter bee** — Long-lived worker phenotype produced for overwintering in temperate climates, characterised by physiological adaptations supporting extended survival.

**Worker bee** — Normally non-reproductive female honey bee performing brood care, comb building, guarding, foraging, thermoregulation, and other colony tasks.

## X–Z

**Xeric** — Describing dry environmental conditions; relevant to forage availability, nectar secretion, and habitat ecology.

**Yeast** — Single-celled fungus; osmotolerant yeasts are important in honey fermentation.

**Yield** — Quantity of harvestable product obtained per colony, apiary, unit area, unit time, or process batch, depending on context.

**Zone, clean** — Food-processing area with controlled contamination risk and exposed food-contact surfaces.

**Zone, incoming/field-exposed** — Processing area receiving supers, containers, or other materials directly from apiary or transport conditions before entry into cleaner product zones.

## Editorial Terminology Rules

- Use **colony** for the living social unit and **hive** primarily for the physical housing unless context requires ordinary conversational usage.
- Use **apiary** for the managed site containing one or more colonies.
- Use **brood** for eggs, larvae, and pupae collectively.
- Use **disease** only when pathology or clinical effect is intended; use **infection** when pathogen presence is the point.
- Use **parasite**, **pest**, **pathogen**, and **predator** according to their biological meanings rather than interchangeably.
- Use **Varroa** as the practical common name but *Varroa destructor* for scientific identification.
- Use **Nosema** in practical discussion while acknowledging the *Vairimorpha* nomenclature where taxonomic precision matters.
- Use **honey moisture** for refractometric water percentage and **water activity** only for the distinct thermodynamic measurement.
- Use **legal maximum/minimum** only for a dated jurisdiction-specific rule; do not present it as an operational target automatically.
- Use **organic** only for a certified or legally defined production system when making a market claim.
- Use **natural** descriptively, never as a synonym for safe, effective, residue-free, or legally authorised.
