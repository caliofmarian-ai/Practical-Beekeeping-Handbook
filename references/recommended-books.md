# Reference 77 — Recommended Books

This list is deliberately selective. It is not a catalogue of every beekeeping title in print. Each recommendation has a defined use, and older classics are clearly distinguished from current practical guidance. Disease treatments, medicine authorisations, food law, and legal requirements must always be checked against current competent-authority sources regardless of a book’s reputation.

## Best First Books for New Beekeepers

### *The Beekeeper’s Handbook* — Diana Sammataro and Alphonse Avitabile
**Best for:** beginners who want one structured, practical reference covering equipment, biology, seasonal work, inspections, swarming, health, and honey production.

**Why it remains valuable:** clear illustrations and a systematic teaching approach. Use current local guidance for medicines, pests, and legal requirements.

### *Honey Bee Biology and Beekeeping* — Dewey M. Caron and Lawrence J. Connor
**Best for:** readers who want practical management tied closely to bee biology.

**Why it remains valuable:** bridges classroom-level biology and real apiary decision-making better than many purely practical manuals.

### *First Lessons in Beekeeping* — Keith S. Delaplane
**Best for:** concise introductory study and preparation before handling the first colony.

**Why it remains valuable:** compact enough to read before the season while still grounding management in biological principles.

## Comprehensive Reference Works

### *The Hive and the Honey Bee* — edited by Joe M. Graham / Dadant & Sons
**Best for:** serious intermediate and advanced beekeepers, instructors, and readers needing a broad technical reference.

**Strength:** multi-author treatment of bee biology, equipment, management, products, health, and industry topics.

**Caution:** editions span long periods; treatment and regulatory guidance should be checked against current sources.

### *The ABC and XYZ of Bee Culture* — A. I. Root / current edition
**Best for:** encyclopaedic lookup, terminology, historical perspective, and practical context.

**Strength:** one of the foundational reference traditions in modern apiculture.

**Caution:** older editions contain obsolete treatment, safety, and management advice. Use recent editions and cross-check modern health guidance.

## Honey Bee Biology and Colony Organisation

### *The Biology of the Honey Bee* — Mark L. Winston
**Best for:** deep understanding of anatomy, development, behaviour, reproduction, communication, and colony organisation.

**Why it matters:** management makes more sense when the reader understands what the colony is biologically trying to do.

### *The Wisdom of the Hive* — Thomas D. Seeley
**Best for:** advanced understanding of colony-level regulation, foraging, resource allocation, and social organisation.

**Why it matters:** shows how individual worker decisions generate colony-level behaviour.

### *Honeybee Ecology: A Study of Adaptation in Social Life* — Thomas D. Seeley
**Best for:** ecology, nest-site biology, population density, foraging, and the relationship between wild colony biology and managed systems.

### *Honeybee Democracy* — Thomas D. Seeley
**Best for:** swarm decision-making, communication, and collective choice.

**Why it matters to practice:** provides an unusually clear model of how scout bees evaluate and select nest sites.

### *The Lives of Bees* — Thomas D. Seeley
**Best for:** comparing managed beekeeping systems with the ecology of wild-living honey bee colonies.

**Use with care:** the book stimulates useful management questions but should not be interpreted as a universal instruction to copy wild nest conditions in every commercial or disease-regulated setting.

### *The Buzz about Bees: Biology of a Superorganism* — Jürgen Tautz
**Best for:** accessible advanced biology with strong visual explanation of the colony as a superorganism.

## Anatomy

### *Anatomy and Dissection of the Honeybee* — H. A. Dade
**Best for:** detailed practical anatomy, microscopy, teaching, and diagnostic study.

### *Anatomy of the Honey Bee* — R. E. Snodgrass
**Best for:** foundational morphological reference.

**Caution:** terminology is historically important but some modern anatomical interpretations and names have evolved.

## Queen Rearing and Breeding

### *Queen Rearing and Bee Breeding* — Harry H. Laidlaw Jr. and Robert E. Page Jr.
**Best for:** queen biology, controlled queen production, breeding principles, mating systems, and selection.

### *Bee Genetics and Breeding* — edited by Thomas E. Rinderer
**Best for:** advanced readers studying genetics, selection, population biology, and breeding programmes.

**Caution:** important foundational material; supplement with modern Varroa-resistance and genomic-selection research.

## Bee Health, Varroa, and IPM

### *Tools for Varroa Management: A Guide to Effective Varroa Sampling & Control* — Honey Bee Health Coalition, 9th edition, 2026
**Best for:** current Varroa sampling, integrated management, treatment decision-making, and resistance-aware control in its target region.

**Why it is essential:** unlike a fixed textbook chapter, this guide is periodically updated as thresholds, products, resistance, and evidence change.

**Regional caution:** product authorisations and thresholds differ; always check the competent authority where the bees are kept.

### COLOSS BEEBOOK volumes and methods papers
**Best for:** researchers, technical advisers, and advanced beekeepers who need standardised experimental and diagnostic methods.

**Format:** research-method collection rather than a conventional single-volume practical textbook.

## Foraging, Nectar, and Pollination

### *Nectaries and Nectar* — edited by Susan W. Nicolson, Massimo Nepi, and Ettore Pacini
**Best for:** advanced plant–pollinator physiology, nectar production, floral biology, and ecological context.

### *The Social Organization of Honeybees* — J. B. Free
**Best for:** classic behavioural and social organisation research.

### IPBES *Assessment Report on Pollinators, Pollination and Food Production*
**Best for:** broad global evidence on wild and managed pollinators, drivers of decline, agriculture, and conservation.

**Format:** assessment report rather than conventional beekeeping book.

## History and Global Beekeeping

### *The World History of Beekeeping and Honey Hunting* — Eva Crane
**Best for:** serious historical study of honey hunting, hive development, cultural practices, and the global history of apiculture.

### *Bees and Beekeeping: Science, Practice and World Resources* — Eva Crane
**Best for:** historical-global technical reference and understanding how beekeeping systems vary around the world.

**Caution:** invaluable as a reference, but legal, medicine, disease, and commercial information should be updated from current sources.

## Honey, Beeswax, and Hive Products

### FAO — *Value-added Products from Beekeeping*
**Best for:** beeswax, propolis, pollen, royal jelly, venom, and value-added product concepts.

**Strength:** broad international perspective.

**Caution:** product regulation, cosmetics law, supplement claims, and food-safety systems have evolved; use modern legal sources for commercial production.

### International Honey Commission analytical resources
**Best for:** honey-quality professionals, packers, laboratories, and advanced producers.

**Format:** harmonised methods and technical guidance rather than a narrative beekeeping book.

## Honey Processing and Quality

For extraction, processing, storage, authenticity, and food hygiene, no single general beekeeping book should be treated as the final authority. Serious producers should use a combination of:

- the current Codex *Standard for Honey*;
- current national/EU honey law where applicable;
- International Honey Commission methods;
- current food-hygiene guidance;
- specialised peer-reviewed honey-quality literature.

This is preferable to relying on old processing recipes or folklore thresholds.

## Organic Beekeeping and Sustainability

Organic production is regulated differently across markets. Rather than recommending one general “organic beekeeping book” as universal, readers should use:

- the current organic regulation/certification standard for their market;
- certification-body operating guidance;
- current Varroa/IPM evidence;
- pollinator-conservation and biodiversity guidance appropriate to the landscape.

A book describing “natural” or “treatment-free” beekeeping is not automatically a guide to certified organic production.

## Business and Commercial Beekeeping

Commercial beekeeping economics vary strongly by market, labour, pollination demand, climate, and regulation. Useful business reading should therefore be paired with current local information on:

- food-business registration;
- tax;
- insurance;
- employment;
- product liability;
- consumer law;
- labelling;
- online sales;
- trade marks.

General small-business accounting and cost-control books can be more useful than old beekeeping-specific profit claims.

## Research and Evidence Appraisal

### *Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions* — current version
**Best for:** understanding evidence synthesis, bias, meta-analysis, certainty, and the difference between one study and a body of evidence.

### PRISMA 2020 Statement and Explanation
**Best for:** understanding how systematic reviews should be reported and how to judge search completeness and selection transparency.

### CONSORT, STROBE, and ARRIVE guideline resources
**Best for:** learning what high-quality reporting should contain in randomised trials, observational studies, and animal research.

These are not beekeeping books, but they are valuable for Chapter 73’s goal: helping beekeepers judge scientific claims instead of accepting them because they appear in a paper.

## Reading Path by Experience Level

### New beekeeper
1. *First Lessons in Beekeeping*.
2. *The Beekeeper’s Handbook*.
3. The relevant national bee-health and food-law guidance.
4. This handbook’s Chapters 15–38 for apiary setup and management.

### Intermediate beekeeper
1. *Honey Bee Biology and Beekeeping*.
2. *The Hive and the Honey Bee*.
3. *The Wisdom of the Hive*.
4. Current HBHC/COLOSS/competent-authority Varroa resources.

### Queen rearer / breeder
1. *Queen Rearing and Bee Breeding*.
2. COLOSS queen-rearing/instrumental-insemination methods.
3. Modern Varroa-resistance and genomic-selection papers.

### Commercial honey producer
1. *The Hive and the Honey Bee* for broad management.
2. Codex and local honey regulations.
3. International Honey Commission methods.
4. Food-hygiene and traceability guidance.
5. Current market-specific business and insurance information.

### Research-oriented beekeeper
1. *The Biology of the Honey Bee*.
2. *The Wisdom of the Hive*.
3. COLOSS BEEBOOK.
4. Current peer-reviewed reviews and meta-analyses.
5. Cochrane/PRISMA/CONSORT/STROBE resources for evidence appraisal.

## Books to Use with Historical Caution

Older classics can be exceptional sources for bee behaviour, traditional hive systems, history, and observation, while simultaneously containing obsolete recommendations for disease treatment, pesticides, medicines, heating, food handling, or equipment safety.

When reading any older beekeeping text:

1. keep biological observations separate from old treatment prescriptions;
2. verify all medicines against current authorised-product information;
3. verify disease-reporting requirements;
4. verify honey composition and labelling law;
5. verify modern understanding of Varroa-virus biology;
6. verify occupational and food safety.

## Selection Rule for Future Editions

A book should remain in this recommended list only if it provides at least one of the following:

- unusually clear instruction;
- enduring foundational science;
- comprehensive reference value;
- specialist depth not duplicated elsewhere;
- important historical context;
- an actively maintained current technical resource.

Popularity alone is not enough.
