## 1. Photograph Selection Rules

Use a photograph when the reader benefits from seeing:

- authentic surface texture or colour;
- natural variation among colonies/products/sites;
- a real disease or pest presentation;
- a field setup whose context matters;
- actual wear/damage/contamination;
- a practical body position or handling setup;
- product grading appearance;
- landscape/habitat conditions.

Prefer illustration/diagram instead when:

- anatomy must be labelled internally;
- a process must show hidden stages;
- exact geometry matters;
- several alternative states need controlled comparison;
- a dangerous procedure would be unsafe to stage;
- a photograph would be cluttered or ambiguous.

## 11. People, Consent, and Privacy

When identifiable people appear:

- obtain model consent appropriate to commercial publication;
- avoid identifiable minors unless essential and properly authorised;
- obscure private vehicle plates, addresses, phone numbers, order labels, customer records, and health information;
- avoid publishing precise coordinates of valuable/remote apiaries when theft/biosecurity risk exists;
- do not imply endorsement by a person or organisation without permission.

---

## 12. Property and Location Permissions

Photographs on farms, laboratories, food premises, businesses, protected sites, or private land require appropriate permission where necessary.

For regulated/certified operations, captions must not imply official approval beyond the actual status documented.

---

## 13. Editing Rules

Allowed routine edits:

- exposure/white balance;
- crop/straighten;
- dust-spot removal that is not diagnostic content;
- background distraction reduction where it does not alter evidence;
- privacy redaction;
- annotation overlays kept as separate production layers.

Not allowed:

- adding/removing disease signs;
- moving mites or eggs;
- making brood patterns more dramatic;
- changing honey colour to fit a varietal claim;
- removing contamination in a photograph used to teach contamination;
- compositing separate events into one “documentary” photo without disclosure.

Diagnostic photos are evidence-like teaching assets and must preserve reality.

---

## 14. Technical Image Requirements

For final print photographs:

- source resolution sufficient for intended print dimensions, normally targeting effective ~300 ppi at placement size;
- retain original high-resolution master;
- use non-destructive editing workflow;
- colour-manage consistently during layout;
- avoid aggressive sharpening/compression;
- test dark brood and microscope images in print proof;
- retain crop flexibility for EPUB/print variants.

Do not distribute proprietary font files or unlicensed source files with the repository.

---

## 15. Caption Rules

A strong caption answers:

- what is visible;
- why it matters;
- what the reader should **not** conclude if the sign is non-specific.

Example:

> Deformed wings on a newly emerged worker are strongly associated with Varroa–DWV damage in many colonies, but the visible phenotype does not quantify current Varroa infestation. Perform validated mite monitoring.

Avoid captions such as “Sick bee” or “Healthy hive” with no diagnostic value.

---

## 16. Rights Priority

Preferred sources:

1. original project photography;
2. commissioned photography with full commercial rights;
3. government/university/institutional imagery with explicit compatible licence/permission;
4. commercial licensing only when unique material cannot reasonably be created.

For every non-original image record licence, attribution, modifications, and permitted formats/territories.
