# Reference 78 — Beekeeping Organizations

This section lists organisations that provide recognised scientific, regulatory, industry, educational, food-safety, conservation, or standards functions relevant to beekeeping. It is intentionally selective. Local associations can be extremely valuable for mentoring and practical training, but their advice should not override current competent-authority rules for disease, veterinary medicines, food safety, or legal compliance.

Websites were checked during the 2026 manuscript cycle and should be re-verified before each new edition.

---

## International Apiculture and Bee-Science Organisations

### Apimondia — International Federation of Beekeepers’ Associations
**Scope:** global.

**Role:** international federation connecting national beekeeping organisations, scientists, industry specialists, and apiculture professionals. It operates scientific commissions and convenes international congresses.

**Useful for:** global apiculture networks, conferences, scientific/technical commissions, policy discussions, and international bee-product topics.

**Official website:** https://apimondia.org/

### COLOSS — Prevention of Honey Bee Colony Losses
**Scope:** global research network.

**Role:** international honey bee research association focused on colony losses, pests, pathogens, management, monitoring, and standardised research methods.

**Useful for:** the COLOSS BEEBOOK, colony-loss surveys, Varroa and Tropilaelaps work, standard research protocols, and current bee-health science.

**Official website:** https://coloss.org/

### International Bee Research Association (IBRA)
**Scope:** international.

**Role:** promotes bee science and beekeeping information worldwide and is associated with major apicultural publications, including the *Journal of Apicultural Research* and *Bee World*.

**Useful for:** scientific literature, specialist publications, research communication, books, and education.

**Official website:** https://ibra.org.uk/

### International Honey Commission (IHC)
**Scope:** international honey and bee-product science network.

**Role:** develops and harmonises analytical approaches for honey and other bee products and contributes to international quality/authenticity science.

**Useful for:** honey-analysis methods, authenticity, unifloral honey characterisation, bee-product quality, and laboratory networks.

**Official website:** https://www.ihc-platform.net/

### Honey Bee Health Coalition
**Scope:** primarily North American agricultural systems, with broader technical value.

**Role:** multi-stakeholder coalition involving beekeepers, growers, researchers, government, industry, conservation organisations, and other partners.

**Useful for:** current Varroa-management guidance, hive-health best practices, forage/nutrition resources, and crop-pest stewardship.

**Official website:** https://honeybeehealthcoalition.org/

## International Standards, Animal Health, and Food Organisations

### World Organisation for Animal Health (WOAH)
**Scope:** intergovernmental/global.

**Role:** sets international animal-health standards and maintains Terrestrial Code/Manual resources for listed honey bee diseases and infestations.

**Useful for:** international context on AFB, EFB, varroosis, Tropilaelaps, small hive beetle, acarapisosis, diagnostics, movement, and reporting frameworks.

**Official website:** https://www.woah.org/

### Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO)
**Scope:** global.

**Role:** agriculture, food systems, rural development, pollination, and beekeeping development.

**Useful for:** beekeeping development, pollination, value-added hive products, food systems, and international agricultural context.

**Official website:** https://www.fao.org/

### Codex Alimentarius Commission
**Scope:** international food standards.

**Role:** FAO/WHO food-standards programme.

**Useful for:** the *Standard for Honey*, general food-hygiene principles, contaminants, food-labelling frameworks, and international trade reference.

**Official website:** https://www.fao.org/fao-who-codexalimentarius/

### International Organization for Standardization (ISO)
**Scope:** international standards.

**Role:** develops voluntary international standards.

**Useful for:** bee-product specifications such as bee pollen, raw propolis, and royal jelly; laboratory competence; quality-management frameworks.

**Official website:** https://www.iso.org/

## European Union Scientific and Regulatory Resources

### European Union Reference Laboratory for Bee Health — ANSES
**Scope:** European Union reference-laboratory network.

**Role:** harmonises and supports official laboratory diagnosis for designated bee-health hazards, including AFB and important parasitic/exotic pests.

**Useful for:** diagnostic methods, reference-laboratory updates, Tropilaelaps and small-hive-beetle surveillance, and EU laboratory networks.

**Official website:** https://sitesv2.anses.fr/en/minisite/abeilles/welcome-website-eu-rl-bee-health

### European Food Safety Authority (EFSA)
**Scope:** European Union scientific risk assessment.

**Role:** independent scientific advice on food/feed safety, pesticides, animal health, contaminants, and related risks.

**Useful for:** pesticide-risk science, food contaminants, bee-health risk assessments, and scientific opinions supporting EU policy.

**Official website:** https://www.efsa.europa.eu/

### European Commission
**Scope:** European Union.

**Role:** EU legislation, policy, implementing/delegated acts, food law, organic production, veterinary medicines, animal health, trade, and consumer regulation.

**Useful for:** current legal texts and authoritative policy context rather than informal summaries.

**Official website:** https://commission.europa.eu/

### European Beekeeping Association (EBA)
**Scope:** European beekeeper network.

**Role:** represents beekeeper interests and supports cooperation on apiculture and bee-product quality.

**Useful for:** European sector perspectives, conferences, and beekeeper-industry issues.

**Website:** verify current official EBA domain before publication because organisational web addresses can change.

## Ireland — Government, Food Safety, Conservation, and Beekeeper Organisations

### Department of Agriculture, Food and the Marine (DAFM)
**Scope:** Republic of Ireland.

**Role:** competent government department for agriculture, animal health, veterinary medicines, bee-health measures, organic production, and related regulation.

**Useful for:** beekeeper/apiary requirements, animal health, notifiable disease, veterinary medicines, organic rules, movement/import issues, and official agriculture information.

**Official website:** https://www.gov.ie/en/organisation/department-of-agriculture-food-and-the-marine/

### Food Safety Authority of Ireland (FSAI)
**Scope:** Republic of Ireland.

**Role:** national food-safety authority providing guidance and coordinating food-law enforcement.

**Useful for:** honey composition/labelling, food-business registration, hygiene/HACCP, allergens, traceability, recalls, online food sales, and the 2026 honey-origin rules.

**Official website:** https://www.fsai.ie/

### National Biodiversity Data Centre
**Scope:** Ireland.

**Role:** national biodiversity data, monitoring, citizen science, and coordination of major biodiversity programmes.

**Useful for:** wild-pollinator monitoring, biodiversity evidence, species records, and conservation resources.

**Official website:** https://biodiversityireland.ie/

### All-Ireland Pollinator Plan
**Scope:** island of Ireland; voluntary framework.

**Role:** evidence-based pollinator-conservation programme managed by the National Biodiversity Data Centre.

**Useful for:** habitat actions for wild bees, hoverflies, moths, farmland, businesses, public land, transport corridors, communities, and the 2026–2030 “pockets of nature every 200 metres” landscape vision.

**Official website:** https://pollinators.ie/

### Federation of Irish Beekeepers’ Associations (FIBKA)
**Scope:** Ireland.

**Role:** federation of local beekeeping associations providing education, member support, representation, examinations, and beekeeping resources.

**Useful for:** finding local associations, training, mentoring, practical education, meetings, and member services.

**Official website:** https://irishbeekeeping.ie/

### Irish Beekeepers’ Association CLG (IBA)
**Scope:** Ireland.

**Role:** national beekeeping membership organisation supporting education, practical beekeeping, bee health, and beekeeper interests.

**Useful for:** training, events, association support, practical information, and community connections.

**Official website:** https://irishbeekeepers.ie/

### Teagasc
**Scope:** Republic of Ireland.

**Role:** Agriculture and Food Development Authority providing agricultural research, advisory, education, and training services.

**Useful for:** farm context, environmental management, business planning, land use, and agricultural advisory resources relevant to apiary sites and pollination relationships.

**Official website:** https://www.teagasc.ie/

## United Kingdom and Northern Ireland

### National Bee Unit (NBU) / BeeBase
**Scope:** England and Wales, with resources of broader technical value; statutory structures differ elsewhere in the UK.

**Role:** government bee-health service providing inspection, surveillance, statutory pest/disease information, training, and BeeBase resources.

**Useful for:** AFB/EFB, exotic pests, inspection resources, pest/disease identification, and current statutory guidance.

**Official website:** https://www.nationalbeeunit.com/

### Department of Agriculture, Environment and Rural Affairs (DAERA), Northern Ireland
**Scope:** Northern Ireland.

**Role:** agriculture, animal health, environment, and statutory bee-health responsibilities.

**Useful for:** Northern Ireland foulbrood guidance, bee-health regulation, reporting, and local agricultural rules.

**Official website:** https://www.daera-ni.gov.uk/

### Agri-Food and Biosciences Institute (AFBI)
**Scope:** Northern Ireland with wider scientific relevance.

**Role:** laboratory, research, and diagnostic services in agriculture and biosciences.

**Useful for:** honey bee pest/disease testing and laboratory information.

**Official website:** https://www.afbini.gov.uk/

### British Beekeepers Association (BBKA)
**Scope:** England and affiliated networks.

**Role:** beekeeper education, examinations, local associations, public engagement, and practical resources.

**Useful for:** training and association-level practical support.

**Official website:** https://www.bbka.org.uk/

### Bee Improvement and Bee Breeders Association (BIBBA)
**Scope:** UK and international interest.

**Role:** honey bee improvement, breeding, local adaptation, and conservation of appropriate bee populations.

**Useful for:** breeding discussions, native/local bees, queen rearing, and genetic improvement.

**Official website:** https://bibba.com/

## How to Use Organisations Correctly

A beekeeper may need several organisations for one problem:

- **local association** for mentoring and practical help;
- **competent authority** for notifiable disease, legal movement, or veterinary medicine;
- **food-safety authority** for honey processing, labelling, and recalls;
- **research network/university** for scientific evidence;
- **standards body/laboratory network** for analytical methods;
- **industry association** for markets, training, advocacy, and professional networking;
- **conservation organisation** for wild-pollinator and habitat work.

No single membership organisation replaces the competent authority.
